## 1. Back to wall

Source:
- CT: "Click the left thumbstick when standing or crouched to put [the hero]'s back to the wall. [The hero] is less visible with his back to a wall." (M-CT p. 9). Xbox: click left stick; PC: Q (W-CTRL).
- So CT has a **crouched** back-to-wall as well as a standing one. Crouch is a separate toggle (B / C) (M-CT p. 9, W-CTRL).
- Same wording and same button in PT (M-PT) and SC1 (M-SC1: "Back to Wall (Q by default). [The hero] is less visible with his back to a wall.").
- The purpose is visibility: "crouching, moving slowly, and keeping your back to the wall also contribute to [the hero]'s invisibility" (M-SC1, M-PT).
- In CT the same button becomes **Switch shoulder** when a weapon is out (M-CT p. 11, W-CTRL).
- CT: drawing and aiming a weapon ends back-to-wall: "When the player upholsters and aims their weapon, they deactivate the cover action and move around freely" (W-COVER).
- PT spy multiplayer (different mode, same studio era): face the wall, press A; move with the stick; B crouches; leave by pressing A again or by drawing the weapon (M-PT).
- PC speed was analogue via the mouse wheel: "Accelerate (mouse wheel up) / Decelerate (mouse wheel down)" (M-SC1); "[The hero]'s walk/run is handled by the forward/backward movement of the mouse wheel" (TE-R).

Not found: wall slide speed, the exact stance (flat back or shoulder), and what happens at the end of a wall in CT.

Unverified (memory): in SC1-CT the hero stands flat with his shoulder blades and the back of his head to the wall, arms down and slightly back. He shuffles sideways, head turned to the direction of travel. At an edge he stops.

## 2. Corners: peek and lean out to shoot

Source:
- SC1: "When [the hero] approaches a corner with his back to the wall, he will automatically peek around the corner. Press Use/Equip while peeking to equip the selected weapon and allow him to shoot around the corner." (M-SC1).
- PT: the same, plus throwing from the peek (M-PT). PT spy mode: keep pushing the stick toward the corner and "he will poke his head around the side of the wall to see what is going on" (M-PT).
- **CT removed shooting from back-to-wall.** "the ability to shoot around corners has been removed, although this is balanced by being able to switch the side of [the hero]'s body the gun is on while in a firing position" (W-CT; same in WP-CT). "the ability to shoot while pressed against a wall has been removed" (TE-R).
- CT's replacement is Switch Shoulder: "While in the Aiming mode, click the left thumbstick to switch your weapon from one shoulder to another. That way you will be able to aim at any angle." (M-CT p. 11). Reviews: "quickly switch shoulders by pressing in on the left stick" (GS-R); "switch the over the shoulder view from right to left" (TE-R).
- So in CT you shoot round a corner by standing off the wall, aiming, and putting the gun and camera on the corner side.

Inference:
- The peek is a **head-and-shoulder** move toward the open side. The head turns toward the opening. A head that bends away from the opening (P7) has no basis in any version.
- Since shoulder switch moves the over-the-shoulder camera to the other side (TE-R), the CT camera at a left-hand corner sits over the left shoulder. That is what lets you see round it.

Unverified (memory): during the SC1 / PT back-to-wall peek the camera swings out beyond the corner so the player sees down the far side. Check on footage.

## 3. Low cover and aiming over it

Source:
- CT back-to-wall works crouched (M-CT p. 9). Crouching is "slower, but quieter, and [the hero] is less visible" (M-CT p. 9).
- CT holds a crouch frame with only a slight breathing motion rather than switching to an idle (80LV, quoting artist Thomas Ripoll Kobayashi).
- Wiki photo caption: "[The hero] fires while taking cover behind barrels" (W-COVER). It does not say whether he is crouched or standing.

Not found: any CT rule for aiming over low cover (auto-rise, pop-up height). CT has no cover system beyond back-to-wall. Cover snapping came with Conviction and Blacklist (W-COVER).

Inference: in CT, aiming from a crouch behind low cover means aiming crouched. Whether the shot clears depends on the cover height. A full stand on aim is not CT behaviour.

## 4. SWAT turn

Source:
- Added in PT: "When clinging to a wall near an open doorway, [the hero] can do a 'swat turn' manoeuvre to move past the open door while remaining hidden from view." (W-PT).
- PT manual: "While [the hero] is peeking at an open door or a small corridor, press the A button to perform a SWAT Turn." (M-PT). PC: Space / Enter (W-CTRL).
- **Not in console / PC CT.** "The SWAT turn was taken out of [the hero]'s repertoire." (GS-R). The wiki lists PT, CT DS and Essentials as the only SWAT-turn games (W-PT). The CT control tables have no SWAT entry (W-CTRL).

## 5. Doors

Source:
- CT door interactions: "open door, open door stealth, bash door, and optic cable" (TE-R). The FAQ also lists SLIDE OPEN, BREAK OPEN, BREAK LOCK and PICK LOCK (GR-FAQ).
- **Open Door Stealth (CT):** "If you want to make sure no one hears you, open the door in a stealthy way. You can make [the hero] open/close the door as fast as you want by playing with the left thumbstick." (M-CT p. 11). "Stealth allows the player to manually push the door ajar being careful of not disturbing whoever's inside." (TE-R).
- **Door Peek (SC1):** "To peek through a door before entering, select the Peek Door interaction. Move Forward to go through the door or move Backward close it." (M-SC1).
- **Bash Door (CT):** "[The hero] will break through to another room by hitting the door with his feet... this move will make some noise, but it can knock out an enemy on the other side." (M-CT p. 11). "Bashing will result in a one-hit takedown of the foe." (GS-R).
- **Break Lock (CT):** knife; "this is noisy, and broken locks will cause guards to become curious." (M-CT p. 11).
- **Lock picking (CT):** rotate the left stick until the pad rumbles, then hold it steady for a few seconds per pin. Quiet and cannot be detected (M-CT pp. 11, 19).
- **Optic cable:** "used to peek under doors ... without being exposed"; CT can cycle normal, night, thermal and EMF views (W-OPTIC). In CT it is an interaction on the door (W-PT: "simply approach a door and select to use your optic cable or lockpick").
- **AI and doors:** CT guards notice "doors being left open" (W-CT).

Inference: SC1's door peek (forward opens, back closes) and CT's open door stealth (stick sets the speed, open or close) are the same model. The door angle follows the stick, so a slow push makes a slow open. This is what P10 asks for.

## 6. Lean in general

Source:
- No lean key in any SC1, PT or CT control table on Xbox, PC, PS2 or GameCube (W-CTRL; the only "lean" in the series pages is enemy AI: CT guards "lean and shoot around corners", W-CT).
- CT's equivalents are switching shoulders while aiming (M-CT) and the back-to-wall head peek.

## 7. How real operators handle walls and corners

Source:
- Don't hug walls: "it is never a good idea to hug the wall, or corner. Bullets can ricochet down the wall" ([Shooting Illustrated](https://www.shootingillustrated.com/content/the-quick-peek-no-thanks)). Stay about an arm's length off the wall when clearing ([Police1](https://www.police1.com/suspect-pursuit/articles/tactical-cornering-during-foot-pursuits-n1HSFpjmptAn0AdD)).
- Keep back from the corner and slice the pie in small sideways steps: "Stay back several feet from the corner or door" ([NRA Women](https://www.nrawomen.com/content/doors-and-corners)); "incrementally move and look so you increasingly see what is around the corner, little by little" ([Athlon](https://athlonoutdoors.com/article/clearing-corners-tips/)).
- Expose little and lean, rather than stepping out: "only expose enough of your body to take a look, usually just a portion of your head"; "With the gun at ready, you lean out to take a look"; "let the muzzle of your gun go where your eyes go" ([Shooting Illustrated](https://www.shootingillustrated.com/content/the-quick-peek-no-thanks)). Lean "your torso slightly so your entire body isn't exposed"; don't lead with the head alone ([NRA Women](https://www.nrawomen.com/content/doors-and-corners)).
- Switch shoulder at a support-side corner to cut exposure; leaning out to make the shot "comes at the cost of major body exposure" ([Athlon, shoulder transition](https://athlonoutdoors.com/article/shoulder-transition-with-rifle/)).
- Low cover: go to one knee. Shooting over the top, the strong-side knee is down; shooting round the side, the knee on that side is down; "The greater the lean, the more you extend your leg" ([Julie Golob](https://www.juliegolob.com/shooting-tip-shooting-from-kneeling-in-idpa), an IDPA competition tip, not doctrine). Kneeling is "high enough to shoot over some taller oddities and low enough to get under some" ([Soldier Systems](https://soldiersystems.net/2016/11/12/gunfighter-moment-jeff-gonzales-7/)).
- Doors: push the door so it swings fully open; don't stand in the doorway; pie the doorway from back and to the side ([NRA Women](https://www.nrawomen.com/content/doors-and-corners)).

Inference: a believable figure on a wall stands **bladed**, angled 30-60 degrees to the wall, not square to it. The wall-side shoulder and upper back may rest on it, the knees are bent, and the head and weapon lead toward the edge. Nobody flattens his whole back and the back of his head to a wall to move along it. That pose is a game convention for low visibility.

## Implications for Night Shift

Authority: Michael > bible (CLAUDE.md). The CT facts below say what CT did; Michael's notes decide what Night Shift does.

| Note | What CT and real practice say | Suggested change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P6 Wall enter (stance) | CT is literally "back to the wall", standing or crouched (M-CT). Real operators don't hug walls; they stand bladed and lean (Sec. 7). | Replace the flat-back pose with a **bladed shoulder-to-wall** stance (see recommendation). Crouched: a low sideways crouch, wall-side shoulder on the wall, head below the cover top. |
| P7 Wall edge (peek, camera) | The peek is head and shoulder toward the opening (M-SC1, M-PT). CT's camera follows the shoulder side (M-CT, TE-R). | The head and neck **yaw and lean toward the open side only**. Clamp them so they never turn back over the wall-side shoulder. On a peek, move the camera pivot **sideways past the edge** and onto the edge-side shoulder so the far side is in view. Exact distance is a tuning value; CT's is unverified. |
| P8 Wall crouch (ends, aim over) | CT aims crouched; no auto-stand (Sec. 3). Real: one knee down, rise only enough for eyes and muzzle to clear (Sec. 7). | Aim over low cover = rise to a **high kneel or half crouch**: eyes and muzzle just over the cover top (tuned per cover height), hips stay low. At the ends, lean out **sideways** from the crouch with the edge-side knee down and the other leg extended for balance. |
| P9 Wall lean (clipping, distance) | CT has no lean-out from the wall; you aim with switched shoulders off the wall (W-CT, M-CT). Real: lean the torso out from the hips; muzzle goes where the eyes go (Sec. 7). | Keep a body gap from the wall (inference: about 10-15 cm on the shoulder, more at the hips) so nothing clips. Pivot the lean about the **outer foot**, away from the wall. Lean far enough that eye and muzzle clear the edge. Put the weapon on the edge-side shoulder, as CT's switch shoulder and the real shoulder transition do. |
| P10 Door peek | SC1: forward goes through, back closes (M-SC1). CT: the stick sets how fast the door opens or closes (M-CT). CT guards notice open doors (W-CT). | The door angle follows the stick: forward pushes it open slowly, back pulls it shut slowly, release holds it. Noise and guard reaction scale with door speed. Keep bash and optic cable as separate choices. |

Other points for P14 ("feel exactly like Chaos Theory"):
- Night Shift has a **SWAT turn** and **lean-out-and-aim from the wall edge** (STATE.md play checklist). Neither is in console or PC CT: CT cut both (GS-R, W-CT, TE-R). Both are PT features. Keeping them is Michael's call; this is a flag, not a recommendation to remove them.
- CT's pattern is: the wall is for hiding and peeking, and drawing to aim takes you off the wall, with a shoulder switch to cover the corner (W-COVER, M-CT). That avoids most wall-clipping problems, because there is no aiming pose on the wall at all.
- The Conviction / Blacklist shoulder-to-cover **pose** is close to what Michael describes. The bible's removal of snap cover is about the mechanic, not the pose: the bladed pose can sit on the existing back-to-wall system without bringing snap cover back (inference).

### Recommendation: wall stance

**Use a sideways shoulder-to-wall stance, not a flat back.** Standing: bladed 30-60 degrees to the wall, wall-side shoulder and upper back touching it, knees soft, weight low, head turned toward the travel or edge side, holstered hands relaxed and close to the body. Crouched: the same in a low crouch, wall-side shoulder on the wall, head below the cover line.

Reasons:
1. Michael asked for it (P6: "more of a sideways crouch with back/shoulder on the wall"), and he ranks above the CT reference.
2. Real practice is against flattening on walls and in favour of a bladed stance that leans out (Sec. 7 sources). A flat back is a game convention.
3. It fixes P7 and P9 at the source. A bladed body already faces the edge, so the head and weapon lead round the corner on the correct side, the lean pivots out from the hips without the back scraping the wall, and the camera's edge-side shoulder offset matches the body.
4. Gameplay stays the same. CT's back-to-wall benefit is lower visibility (M-CT), and Night Shift gameplay reads collision, occupancy and light, not pose (CLAUDE.md). Changing the pose doesn't change the stealth rules.
5. It reuses the existing wall state, slide and edge logic. Only the pose, the head clamp, the camera offset and the lean pivot change.
