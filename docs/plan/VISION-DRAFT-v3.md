# Night Shift - Vision (draft v3, 2026-11-02)
Owner: Michael. Status: draft for Michael's approval; on approval it replaces `docs/VISION.md` (history).

From draft v2 and Michael's decisions since (N1-N37), the master brief's formula (section 3) and the Hazelight
research (`docs/research/hazelight-2026-11/README.md`).

## 1. What it is
A 2-player co-op third-person stealth campaign. Two operators of a deniable unit, Wren and Moth, slip through places
built to stop one person. Inspired by Chaos Theory's co-op campaign, with our own ideas where they make it more fun.
Online co-op and local split-screen. Sold on Steam (Windows first, Steam Deck a goal once tested) with a free
Friend's Pass, so only one of the two needs to buy it. Nothing else: no solo play, no bot partner, no other modes.

## 2. The formula
- **Chaos Theory's co-op stealth sandbox:** light and shadow, readable guards, team moves that open routes, two-key
  actions, the percent rating, a level that breathes.
- **A Way Out's grounded two-person drama:** story-first missions, quiet moments between tense ones, "distract while
  I work", joint decisions, an ending that uses the bond the players built.
- **It Takes Two's co-op craft:** forgiving failure, both players always busy, pair-tested relentlessly, ideas cut fast.
- **Hitman's living world:** context actions on people and objects, overheard talk that opens opportunities, tricks
  played on the world.
- **Not Split Fiction's carousel:** one stealth ruleset all campaign; variety comes from places, people, light,
  situations and mission rules, never from new controls every few minutes.
- **Music that lifts it:** an adaptive score with a recurring signature carried in sound and rhythm.

## 3. The one rule
**Forced co-op teamwork is the game.** If a feature or a space works just as well solo, it is wrong.

## 4. Pillars (ranked; the higher one wins a tie)
1. **Co-op teamwork** - team moves and coordination are part of every route.
2. **Light and shadow** - darkness hides you, light exposes you; the picture, the light meter and the guards agree.
3. **Readable, fair guards** - few guards, each a puzzle you can learn; every detection can be explained.
4. **Always know where to go** - objectives, the compass and the level itself make the next step clear.
5. **Physical, deliberate movement** - weighted and controllable.
6. **Sound and music** - noise matters; the score carries the tension.

## 5. The two operators
- **Same abilities.** Each team moment gives the two players different jobs by where they stand or what they carry,
  and the jobs alternate, so neither is always the one who boosts or holds.
- **Wren**, team lead: terse, carries the team's conscience; when Wren talks at length it matters. **Moth**, climber:
  cheerful, curious, drawn to light. Their banter is dry, reacts to what just happened, goes quiet when guards are
  alert, and never spells out a solution. Both players always get the same story.

## 6. The campaign
- **Marlow Yard prologue** teaches each team move on its own, in a fixed order, each step leaning more on the partner.
- **Seven missions over nine nights** (provisional; final after Mission 1 shows the real cost): Wren and Moth have
  nine nights to stop SUNDOWN, a staged blackout of the port city Hollowmere. A grounded techno-thriller with dry
  humour, told through text radio, documents and overheard talk.
- **Story first, play wins a tie:** each mission starts from why we are here tonight, what is at stake and what
  changes for Wren and Moth; if a step is not fun, the story beat changes, not the stealth rules.
- **The ending is written now** and hinted at from Mission 1: "Two switches" (`docs/plan/ENDING-DRAFT.md`, A). With
  the radio jammed, Wren holds the control room and Moth the roof generator, each the other's eyes; the last press is
  the first two-person action they ever learned, and Moth, drawn to light, turns the city back on.
- Each night has one short, optional quiet moment.

## 7. Missions
- 20-30 minutes for a good pair, 3-5 steps; a small team moment every 2-4 minutes, a set piece every 6-10 minutes with
  a calm stretch after, a twist about halfway, a joint climax, a short exit.
- Every mission has one action both do at once, one "cover me while I work", one place only two bodies can reach,
  one intel step that is used, one overheard conversation that opens an opportunity, and three planned wow moments.
- Each room or yard offers 2-3 ways through: shadow, high route, team move, or a tool, trick or intel route. Splits end
  at a clear meeting point within about 3 minutes. Each area opens on a dark lookout spot with room for two.
- At most one new mission toy and one joint approach choice ("Wren's way" or "Moth's way") per mission.
- The method: `docs/plan/MISSION-TEMPLATE.md`.

## 8. People in the world
- **Guards:** 20-30 per mission, about a Chaos Theory co-op level (typically 3-6 in an area, more in set pieces),
  hand-placed, **never spawned**; each has a job, a routine and a moment alone. An
  alarm makes the guards already there tougher. **When spotted, hiding is the answer:** guards are deadly head-on,
  search where they last saw you, and never know where you are once you are hidden. They come to fix lights.
- **Staff:** 1-3 unarmed people per mission with routines. They react, and can be avoided, questioned or used;
  harming them costs rating. No disguises, no crowds.
- **Context actions:** one button does the right thing at people and objects (open, use, sabotage, hide a body,
  question someone). **World tricks:** cut power, break a pipe, start a machine, stage an accident, throw something to
  pull a guard away.

## 9. Seeing, finding the way, talking
- The picture is lifted so dark areas stay readable; night vision is for very dim spots and never blows out.
- **Compass bar:** the objective with its distance, and the partner's mark. The level still guides on its own.
- Objectives say plainly what to do and where. Pings show the player's colour (Wren green, Moth purple) with their
  number. Voice chat stays outside the game. A downed partner is impossible to miss. One radio line at a time.

## 10. Rules, failure and score
- One difficulty, tuned well, plus assists.
- Checkpoints at the start of each area and before each set piece. Downed: the partner revives you (30-second window,
  twice per mission). Both down: back to the last checkpoint. A dropped player reconnects at the last checkpoint.
- Alarms never fail a mission; a mission may set up to 2 signposted rules (for example no kills), and breaking one
  restarts the section.
- Kills are allowed unless a mission forbids them and always cost rating unless the mission makes them necessary.
  Each mission ends with a percent rating and its breakdown.
- Players choose their gear before each mission: pistol, rifle, non-lethal gadgets (sticky cam, noisemaker, flashbang,
  sleeping gas, EMP) and lethal gadgets (frag grenade, proximity mine); no drone; the pistol jammer is always carried;
  missions may limit gear. Nothing carries over between missions.

## 11. Look and sound
- Greybox minimum: the Test Chamber look, lit as the real level. Nothing is polished before it passes its playtest.
- Dynamic music: menu, infiltration, caution, alert, with one recurring signature in sound and rhythm (no hummable
  tune). Sound comes first for readability: footsteps per surface, guard barks, partner sounds.

## 12. Not now
Sector Ops, solo play, 3-4 players, other modes, progression, cosmetics, in-game voice, more difficulties, disguises,
crowds, special powers per character or per mission, consoles, Mac and Linux.

## 13. How we build
Nothing is spent beyond Steam's own fees; free assets only under G407, and generated ones are declared. Movement,
co-op and greybox first; play-test before any polish. One main session; GitHub tests every change.
