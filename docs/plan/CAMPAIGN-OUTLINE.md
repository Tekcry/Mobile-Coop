# Night Shift - campaign outline (draft)
Owner: the orchestrator. Status: current (draft from the planning run, 2026-11-02; Michael decides the story with the
missions, STORY-03 / STORY-04).

The story spine from `docs/story.md` mapped onto the three scope tiers (D13), where the twist and the emotional turn
land, and where each team move is taught and reused. Mission 1 is a fresh design (N2): its setting is chosen in level
planning, so "Dead Line" below is the story plan's candidate, not a commitment.

## Story spine (kept if Michael confirms P3)
- **Hook:** someone will switch off Hollowmere. A deniable two-operator unit, NIGHT SHIFT, hears one word on a tapped
  line: SUNDOWN, nine nights away. The team that lives in the dark has to keep the lights on.
- **Two temperaments** (story.md 4): **Wren**, team lead, ex-military, terse, carries the conscience ("when Wren talks
  at length it matters"); **Moth**, climber, ex-steeplejack, cheerful, curious, drawn to light (the running joke).
  Their banter is dry, quiet when guards are alert, back when it is calm. Both move the same (D11 pending).
- **The radio crew:** LANTERN (Peg, the handler), Tally (signals), Sexton (surveillance). Text only (D16).
- **Midpoint reveal:** in Lantern Out, intel proves the Office's own director is the Client (story.md 5).
- **Late turn:** SUNDOWN starts early; the city goes dark mid-mission (Substation Zero).
- **Ending:** the team turns the lights back on; the last image is Hollowmere lighting up block by block, seen from a
  rooftop. Mission 8 and the ending are open (no traitor, no player-versus-player finale, G9).
- **The emotional turn that uses the co-op bond** (brief 5.2), to be chosen with the ending. Candidates:
  1. **Lantern Out, the radio dies:** the players have leaned on LANTERN for five missions; now only each other and the
     pings. The bond is the mechanic. (Already in the story plan.)
  2. **The finale apart:** Wren in grid control, Moth on the substation roof, each the other's only eyes, bringing the
     city back block by block; the last two-key action is the switch that lights the rooftop view.
  3. **Wren speaks:** after a campaign of one-word lines, Wren's one long message, seeded from Mission 1, lands when
     Moth is downed and out of reach (needs the revive rules to allow a scripted "you hold on").

## Team moves: taught, then reused
The kept list comes from Phase 4; this is the plan for the moves most likely to survive. Teaching order (Marlow Yard,
interdependence rising: one helps, then hold and swap, then stranded, then two levels, then simultaneous, then
continuous control): leg-up, jam alternation, partner throw, leg-up and ledge relay, hang-down, a two-key action, rope.

| Move | Taught | Reused (rising difficulty) | Combined with | Frequency per mission |

| Leg-up (boost) | Prologue 1 | Every mission | Ledge relay, throw | 3-5 |
| Jam / light sharing | Prologue 2 | 1, 3, 4 (motion lights), 7 (blackout flips it) | Distract-and-act | 2-4 |
| Partner throw, far-side release | Prologue 3 | 1, 3, 5 | Lure and ambush | 0-2 |
| Ledge relay | Prologue 4 | 1, 4, 6 | Hang-down, rescue | 1-2 |
| Hang-down | Prologue 5 | 3, 5, 6 | Overwatch | about 1 |

| Double drop (joint takedown) | Mission 1 | Every mission with guard pairs | Lure | 1+ |

## Joint approach choice (D12, at most one per mission)
A natural fit for the two temperaments: **Wren's way** (patient, low, on the ground) or **Moth's way** (bold, high,
over the roofs). Both must agree; it changes the route and the play, never the story.
