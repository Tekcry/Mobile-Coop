# Night Shift - the ending (draft for Michael's choice)
Owner: the orchestrator (writes; Michael decides). Status: draft, Phase 1 (N36).

Written now, as Hazelight did for Brothers and A Way Out, so Mission 1 can plant its hints. Fixed by earlier
decisions: co-operative to the last scene, never player against player (G9); both players get the same story; the last
image is Hollowmere lighting up block by block, seen from a rooftop (`docs/story.md` 1). The lesson taken from Brothers
(research `story-process.md` section 4): the ending lives in a control the players have used all game, so nothing new
is learned at the end and the meaning is in how it is used.

## The two arcs
- **Wren** starts as the lead who carries everything and decides alone, in one- and two-word orders. Over the nine
  nights Wren learns to hand the hard calls to Moth. Payoff: Wren's one long message at the end; until then, a writing
  rule keeps every Wren line to a few words.
- **Moth** starts as the team's running joke: a climber drawn to light, a terrible trait in this job. Over the nine
  nights the joke turns into the point: the operator drawn to light is the one who turns the city's lights back on.

## The finale's place
The last mission, Sundown, at the Ashgrove grid control centre while the city is dark. Restoring a dead grid needs two
places at once (in real grids this is a "black start": a local generator is started at the plant while the control
room closes breakers in order), which gives the two-person finale a real-world reason.

## Candidates

### A. Two switches (recommended)
The radio is jammed, as on the night LANTERN was taken: only pings. Wren takes the control room (quiet, guards,
screens that show the breaker order); Moth climbs to the black-start generator house on the roof gantry (height,
light). Each is the other's eyes: Wren pings the order and the guards on the stairs; Moth, high up, sees the guards
heading for the control room and pings them. The last action is the **same two-key "both ready" ring** the pair used
on their first night at the broker's cage, now across the whole site. They press together; the city lights up block
by block; Moth sees it arrive from the gantry; Wren watches it through the control-room window; Wren's one long
message closes the game.
- Uses: split-and-sync, pings only, the two-key action (all taught and reused before). No new controls.
- Cost: lowest; one big lighting moment.

### B. Carry
In the finale's last stretch Wren is hurt and cannot walk (a story event, not a down). Moth carries Wren out with the
body-carry move used on guards all campaign. Carried over Moth's shoulder, Wren faces backwards, so only Wren sees the
guards behind: Wren pings, Moth moves. On the rooftop Moth sets Wren down to watch the lights come on.
- Uses: the familiar carry, given a new meaning (the strongest echo of Brothers).
- Cost: a new carried-player state (look behind, ping, maybe the pistol jammer); one player cannot walk for a few
  minutes, so that player must stay busy.

### C. The radio comes back
The whole last mission runs without radio. When the lights come on, LANTERN's voice returns: "Come home."
- Uses: the handler bond more than the partners' bond.
- Cost: lowest, but the weakest use of the co-op bond.

## Hints Mission 1 plants (for A; B and C differ slightly)
1. The first two-key action (the broker's cage) uses the "both ready" ring, with LANTERN: "Both hands on it."
2. Moth on a roof, looking at the city: a line about the lights nobody looks up at.
3. Wren's rule of few words is visible from the first line.
4. LANTERN's last line of the night: "Then we've nine nights. Come home." (already in `docs/story.md` 8).
5. Later, a found document at the flood barrier explains a black start, so the finale's reason is known before it is
   needed.
