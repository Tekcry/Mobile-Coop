## 0. Release history (shared with UN Headquarters)
- Announced by Ubisoft on 12 May 2005 as free co-op maps for Xbox Live and PC, due "shortly after" the Versus map Squat (end of May). [VERIFIED - Shacknews (12 May 2005); Gaming Nexus (12 May 2005)]
- Re-announced mid-July 2005 alongside the Versus map Steel Squat with a description: a "claustrophobic network of passages" plus "massive open spaces", plenty of loud machinery and demanding puzzles, cooperation key. Due "in the coming weeks", free. [VERIFIED as Ubisoft PR text reprinted by Hexus (15 Jul 2005), MyGamer, VideoGamer - one origin, three outlets]
- Xbox: released on Xbox Live 24 August 2005 (Ubisoft press release dated 24 Aug; Major Nelson's Xbox.com post 25 Aug). Free. Two separate downloads, one mission each, which "continue the Coop story and bring it to the end". Playable in split-screen, System Link and Xbox Live, but ONLY in Mission and Elite modes (not Story). [VERIFIED - Ubisoft press release (via Game Developer/Gamasutra press archive); news.xbox.com Major Nelson post]
- Elite requirement (Xbox): Major Nelson wrote that players must play through Co-op Story mode on Elite to see the maps initially; after that they are unlocked in Co-op Mission mode and selectable any time. [SINGLE SOURCE - news.xbox.com]. Background: per Prima, co-op on Xbox/PC has Story (in order), Mission (any unlocked mission) and Elite (Mission mode with non-lethal weapons only, available after finishing Story). [SINGLE SOURCE - Prima]. Note the press release itself does NOT mention an Elite-completion requirement.
- Launch problems (Xbox): Major Nelson added an edit that there "may be some issues" with the content. GameSpot reported claims the content was unplayable because the two maps did not appear on the level-select screen, and that a Ubisoft forum moderator said it was being looked into. [VERIFIED - Xbox.com edit + GameSpot (GameSpot read only via search snippets; page 403)]. Whether the "missing from level select" reports were simply the Elite gate is not established. [UNCERTAIN]
- Xbox package sizes (from a preservation listing): Nuclear Plant 2090 blocks; UN Headquarters 1990 blocks; the DLC package includes a title update. Original Xbox Live closed 15 April 2010, so the content can no longer be downloaded officially. [SINGLE SOURCE - Xbox-Scene file listing (2023), crediting digiex/Harcroft]
- PC: added by patch v1.05, released 19 October 2005 (66 MB from 1.04; 115 MB full). [VERIFIED - Gamezoom (19 Oct 2005); PCGames.de (19 Oct 2005)]. The patch readme: two new co-op levels (UN Headquarters, Nuclear Plant) selectable in Create Game -> "Mission" mode; NOT accessible in Story mode; do not support the "Use native languages" sound option. [VERIFIED - LoneBullet readme copy; Softpedia/PCGW listing via search snippet]. A PCGames.de reader comment notes Xbox had them about two months earlier. [SINGLE SOURCE]
- PC Elite requirement: the PC readme does not mention one. [Gap]
- Steam: Elder-Geek (Sep 2009) reviewed the Steam re-release and described "five missions" of co-op, not counting these. [SINGLE SOURCE; count ambiguous]
- Order:
  - Xbox: Nuclear Plant is mission 6, UN Headquarters mission 7 (final). [VERIFIED - Fandom (prev/next links: Train Station -> Nuclear Plant -> UN HQ); Ubisoft press release lists Nuclear Plant first; GameSpot calls Nuclear Plant "the first mission" (snippet); Nuclear Plant briefing opens by referring back to Grand Central. Uploads numbered this way (platform not stated): Agt_ Pendergast "[6: Nuclear Plant]", "[7: UN Headquarters]"; BoSstonPL calls UN HQ the "sixth mission" (not counting Training)]
  - PC: UN Headquarters is listed/played before Nuclear Plant, which is the last.   - Caveat: because PC puts them in Mission mode only, "order" means menu/list order; some PC players still play Nuclear first (PleasePlayMore PC #17 Nuclear, #18 UN; Urban Reflex Steam: Part 5 Nuclear, Part 6 UN). [VERIFIED]

## 1. Setting & premise
- Yongbyon nuclear research complex, North Korea; a power plant plus a separate radiochemistry laboratory joined by an external footbridge. In-game date: sometime 18-31 October 2007. [location VERIFIED - Fandom; Ubisoft PR (Hexus); hexen2k7 briefing text. Date SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom]
- Briefing (paraphrased, Xbox order): the Grand Central attack was stopped but Kim escaped. Analysis of the defused bombs shows their three double-laser detonators came from the Yongbyon plant, long suspected by the CIA to be cover for weapons research. Kim could not have done this alone; find out who at Yongbyon helped him and how many detonators he received. [VERIFIED - Fandom briefing; same text in hexen2k7 description (Feb 2009)]
- GameSpot's summary (search snippet): players hunt for a high-ranking Korean officer who has been assisting Kim, who is still at large with one more bomb. [SINGLE SOURCE]
- Narrative payoff (in-mission data): an email from Wen Bae-Su, director of the Radiochemistry Laboratory, dated 10 Oct 2007, records delivering FOUR double-laser detonators to Colonel "Kim Pak-Tu" on orders; an email from Jin Soo (CGIB, Research Department for External Intelligence), dated 8 Oct 2007, asks Wen to send as many detonators as possible to Kim for a "test mission". So: 4 received, 3 used in New York -> Kim can strike again; Jin Soo is the helper. [SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom "Nuclear Plant/Data"; the deduction is mine]. Note the data spells the colonel "Kim Pak-Tu" while the wiki article is "Kim Park-Chu". [SINGLE SOURCE]

## 2. Objective chain
Primary: [VERIFIED - Fandom; hexen2k7 description (Feb 2009) lists the same]
1. Find out who helped Kim.
2. Exfiltrate to the primary extraction point - out of the radiochemistry laboratory's side door.
Secondary: [VERIFIED - Fandom; hexen2k7]
- Discover if Kim can attack again (Kim used 3 bombs in New York; find out how many he got). Fandom places this on two computers in the server room. [placement SINGLE SOURCE]
Opportunity: [VERIFIED - Fandom; hexen2k7]
- Gather the Yongbyon complex's blueprints (first computer); with them Lambert can give advice about the complex. [placement SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom]
Mission notes shown in-game (these function as the level's signposting): [SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom EN/RU]
- The central archives are in the main control room.
- The main control room is near the turbine area.
- A security camera covers the stairs in the turbine area.
- The doors to the main control room are locked by a retinal scanner.
- The radiochemistry lab is connected to the plant by an external footbridge.
- Lasers protect the corridor leading to the footbridge.
- The radiochemistry lab's server room is locked by a retinal scanner.
- Primary extraction is out of the radiochemistry lab's side door.
How objectives update: the notes imply a chain plant -> turbine area -> main control room (central archives) -> laser corridor -> footbridge -> radiochemistry lab -> server room -> side-door extraction. The archive/server computers yield the two emails that answer "who helped" and "can he attack again". Whether objectives are revealed in stages is undocumented. [UNCERTAIN - inference from notes + data]
- Computers listed in the data page, in order: (1) Yongbyon blueprints image; (2) "Open security doors" access; (3) "Stop ventilation fans" access; (4) "Open central archives server" access; (5) Wen Bae-Su email; (6) Jin Soo email. [SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom data]. This suggests the archives are opened from a separate terminal and that fans are a hackable obstacle (see 3).

## 4. Spatial flow
- Ubisoft's design pitch: tight passages alternating with very large open spaces full of loud machinery; puzzles that require cooperation. [Ubisoft PR - one origin, reprinted by 3 outlets]
- Two buildings: power plant (turbine hall with balconies/walkways, stairs, main control room) and radiochemistry laboratory (server room, side-door extraction), joined by an outdoor footbridge reached via a laser corridor. [SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom notes]
- Vertical: the turbine room has at least a floor level and a balcony level with a gap; a ladder and a fan inlet connect levels. [SINGLE SOURCE - Neoseeker snippets]
- Loud machinery should mask noise (Ubisoft PR stresses "loud machines"), but no source documents a noise-masking mechanic here. [UNCERTAIN]
