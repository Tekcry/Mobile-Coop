## 3. Timeline

- **Late 2002 to early 2003:** CT work starts at Montreal after Splinter Cell 1 (Nov 2002). Stated dev length varies: "two and a half years" (Hocking, 2005), "the last two years" (Lepage, 2005), 24 months (Hocking blog, 2015). [VERIFIED - 2+ sources] for roughly 2 to 2.5 years. Working title "Splinter Cell 3" [VERIFIED - Fandom + Wikipedia refs].
- **Early concept:** The project started as single-player + co-op only. Lepage says co-op was at first the main mode of CT, then single-player was prioritised "in terms of market penetration". [SINGLE SOURCE - Globe and Mail launch interview, 15 Apr 2005]
- **Versus added:** Montreal realised it could not ship without adversarial play and asked the PT multiplayer team (Annecy) to build it. [SINGLE SOURCE - same interview; consistent with the Ubisoft PR studio split]
- **E3 2004 (May):** Co-op was shown. GameSpot's E3 impressions describe two spies entering a building, one kneeling to boost the other over a wall, with more emphasis on teamwork than PT. A YouTube upload of "original E3 footage showing off the game's CO-OP mode" exists (SickadtSteve, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IIfns1gvSRY). [VERIFIED - 2+ sources] that co-op was shown at E3 2004. Details of the demo [SINGLE SOURCE - GameSpot snippet].
- **28 Sep 2004:** Ubisoft announced multiplayer details. Co-op would be built by the Montreal team on dedicated maps, with online, system link and split-screen, plus the Versus modes (story, disk hunt, deathmatch). [SINGLE SOURCE - Xbox Addict headline + search snippet; GameSpot "multiplayer update" and Neowin also covered it but were blocked]
- **Late 2004 to early 2005 previews:** Four co-op levels were promised, with about 10 new co-op-specific moves (human ladder, hand boost, tandem rappel, Tomoe Nage throw) and proximity voice that guards can hear (GamesFirst!). A GameSpot "First Look" said five missions including a tutorial. GameSpot did a co-op hands-on of Panama. [VERIFIED - 2+ sources] for the move set and voice feature.
- **Mar 2005:** Hocking gave a GDC 2005 talk on the game's narrative structure, not co-op [SINGLE SOURCE - Hocking blog]. The game shipped in North America 28 Mar 2005 (Xbox/PC/PS2) and 31 Mar (GC) [Fandom infobox; Ubisoft PR dated 28-29 Mar].
- **Launch:** A developer told the Globe and Mail that up to four more co-op levels were being designed for Xbox Live [SINGLE SOURCE]. Only two shipped.
- **12 May 2005:** Ubisoft announced free maps for PC and Xbox Live: the Versus map "Squat" (end of May), then co-op maps "Nuclear Plant" and "UN HQ" [SINGLE SOURCE - Gaming Nexus reprint of Ubisoft release]. Penny Arcade (27 Jul 2005) says the co-op story ends abruptly and two maps would arrive within weeks [consistent]. The brief places release around Aug 2005. Exact date not independently confirmed here.
- **2005:** The Limited Collector's Edition bonus disc included development team video diaries and an exclusive G4 "Making of" (Wikipedia + Fandom). Their content was not retrieved. YouTube uploads exist (see Sources).
- **2011:** The PS3 Classic Trilogy HD dropped co-op and Versus [SINGLE SOURCE - Fandom "Cooperative"; ScreenRant says rereleases forgot co-op = 2 sources -> VERIFIED].
- **2016 / 2022:** PC online was shut down. Steam users date it to 2016 [UNCERTAIN]. CT (PC) appears in Ubisoft's April 2022 decommission list [VERIFIED - VGC + Shacknews].

## 4. Stated design intent and principles (developer or official statements only)

1. **"Three games in one."** Hocking said the team set out to make three games in one box: single-player at the core, plus co-op and Versus. [SINGLE SOURCE - Globe and Mail interview. Reviewers and Ubisoft PR repeat the framing.]
2. **Sam stays the centre, so co-op uses other agents.** Hocking said Sam is the franchise and single-player is the core. Co-op was added so friends could share the fiction, world and gameplay "intimately". [SINGLE SOURCE] No developer statement was found on why the co-op stars two unnamed trainees (Agent One/Two) rather than Sam. The in-fiction reason (Shadownet sends under-trained agents during a crisis) comes from the game and Wikipedia. -> Gap.
3. **Physical co-operation as the fantasy.** Hocking's example: reaching out and pulling a friend in another city up a ledge. He closed by saying you can play with friends who aren't present and believe they're real. [SINGLE SOURCE]
4. **Objectives impossible alone.** The Sept 2004 announcement and the Polish distributor PR (Cenega) pitch co-op as letting two NSA agents achieve goals a single spy couldn't. The Prima guide's intro (Ubisoft-approved) uses the same framing. [VERIFIED - 2+ sources, all marketing or official]
5. **Two kinds of co-op moves.** Generic moves (Boost/Short Scale, Human Ladder, Tomoe Nage) work anywhere. Contextual or "static" moves (Dual Rappelling, Hangover, shoulder-stand) only work at authored spots. One player signals and the other completes the move. [VERIFIED - Prima eGuide + PS2 manual + TeamXbox review]
6. **Voice as a gameplay system.** Headset speech travels positionally, and AI guards can hear it. Players can whisper, mask speech with ambient noise, or deliberately lure guards. Sensitivity is adjustable. [VERIFIED - GamesFirst preview, GameSpot Panama hands-on snippet, TeamXbox review, Steam users]
7. **Accessibility for players without a headset.** Moving into position sends an automatic voice cue to the partner, and synchronized actions are simplified online without a headset. [SINGLE SOURCE - Prima eGuide]
8. **Soft failure for partners.** A downed agent can be revived within about 30 seconds with an adrenaline shot (two each). Agents can share gadgets and sticky-cam views. [Prima eGuide; GameRevolution mentions adrenaline heal -> VERIFIED]
9. **Co-op tech carried into Versus.** In Versus, spies also got co-op moves and could heal and share gear. [VERIFIED - Ubisoft PR, GamesFirst, GameRevolution, TeamXbox]
10. **Platform scope.** Xbox: split-screen, system link, Live. PC: LAN + online, no split-screen. PS2/GC: offline split-screen only. GC had no Versus. [VERIFIED - Ubisoft PR + Prima + GamesFirst]
11. **General CT goals that frame the co-op.** These come from Hocking/Lepage and are not co-op-specific: remove trial-and-error and "Game Over" gating, make low-level choices meaningful (the origin of the name "Chaos Theory"), add the sound/masking meter, and rewrite the AI with navigation meshes. [SINGLE SOURCE - Globe and Mail] Contrast: a reviewer notes co-op missions have no checkpoints (Elder-Geek 2009), and the GC co-op has no mid-mission save (Classic Co-op).

**Not found (no developer statement located):** why missions were built around two-player moves rather than just two players in SP maps; why objectives are split or simultaneous; how AI was tuned for two players (e.g. awareness scaling, difficulty). Reviews describe the results (see reception.md), but intent is undocumented in what I could reach.

## 5. Relation to other Splinter Cell co-op and to Annecy's later work

- **Pandora Tomorrow (2004, Shanghai; Versus by Annecy):** no co-op campaign, only Spies vs Mercs. CT's co-op was new to the series. [VERIFIED - Fandom "Cooperative" + Green Man Gaming + Guinness]
- **Double Agent (2006):** two different games. The old-gen version (Xbox/PS2/GC/Wii, Ubisoft Montreal) has co-op missions tied to the story, again with Agent One and Agent Two (second assignment in 2008, supporting Sam undercover in the JBA). The 360/PS3/PC version (Shanghai, with Milan per Wikipedia) has no story co-op, only co-op challenges. [VERIFIED - Wikipedia DA page + Fandom + ScreenRant + Green Man Gaming] Agent One/Two continuity specifically: [VERIFIED - Fandom + ScreenRant]
- **Conviction (2010, Montreal):** a separate co-op prologue with Archer (Third Echelon) and Kestrel (Voron), 10 days before the campaign, ending with the two players fighting each other, plus Deniable Ops modes. Co-op game director Patrick Redding said stealth was built around Mark & Execute and Last Known Position. Creative director Maxime Beland called CT "very hardcore". [Wikipedia Conviction; Fandom; ScreenRant -> VERIFIED for structure; Redding/Beland quotes SINGLE SOURCE]
- **Blacklist (2013, Toronto lead):** Sam and Briggs co-op missions given by Paladin crew members (14 total, split-screen). Wikipedia says Ubisoft Shanghai developed the co-op mode [SINGLE SOURCE]. Spies vs Mercs returned. A search snippet claims Annecy built Blacklist's multiplayer [UNCERTAIN].
- **Ubisoft Annecy afterwards:** known for the multiplayer of several Splinter Cell games. It later led Steep (2016) and Riders Republic (2021) and supported The Division 2 [SINGLE SOURCE - Wikipedia list of Ubisoft subsidiaries]. No source found linking Annecy to CT's co-op (as opposed to Versus).
- **Fandom wiki:** says co-op appears in CT, DA (v2), Conviction and Blacklist, but not DA v1. Early CT screenshots had both agents with blue goggle lights before red/blue were split. [SINGLE SOURCE]
