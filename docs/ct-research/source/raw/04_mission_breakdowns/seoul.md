## A. Xbox / PC - Seoul 3

### 1. Setting and premise [VERIFIED - FAN + RYB + PRIMA]
- **Setting.** A city under invasion. There is heavy war ambience, and the on-screen notes warn that gunfire is not necessarily aimed at the players. It is the same day as the solo hero's mission in the same city.
- **Task.** Abduct a chemist suspected of making chemical and biological weapons before enemy forces extract him. He must be taken alive, and his death fails the mission.
- **Briefing tone.** The controller calls it an easy job. This sets up the twist.
- **Twist.** The man in the hotel is a body double. The real target has been moved by special forces.
- **Crossover with the solo campaign.**
  - In the solo campaign the hero interrogates a soldier for the trainees. Co-op players hear this exchange over the radio, and the soldier names the new location (a cyber cafe by the fish market). [VERIFIED - FAN transcript, EG, GameGrin, RYB]
  - One walkthrough places the exchange right after the second anti-aircraft vehicle. [SINGLE SOURCE - RYB]
  - The trainees assume the hero's name is a call-sign. [SINGLE SOURCE - FAN]

### 2. Objective chain
**Primaries, in the order they are added** [VERIFIED - FAN + PRIMA + RYB order]:
1. Capture the chemist at a small hotel near the insertion point.
2. Find out where he is (added after the double is revealed).
3. Destroy two mobile anti-aircraft vehicles. This is added at a balcony overlook. [Trigger point SINGLE SOURCE - RYB]
4. Reach the cyber cafe and capture him alive.
5. Carry him to the extraction truck outside the cafe.

**Opportunities:**
- **Interrogate an enemy officer** for the location. Per the guide, doing so also completes primary 2. [VERIFIED for existence; dual completion SINGLE SOURCE - PRIMA]
- **Sabotage the escape boat's engine** at the fish market.
  - An optional computer reveals the boat plan. Players who skip it get no reminder. [VERIFIED computer - FAN + RYB; reminder behaviour SINGLE SOURCE - RYB]
  - If the boat is intact and the chemist flees, he escapes and the mission fails. [VERIFIED - FAN RU + RYB]
  - The launch-era guide does not list this objective. The Russian wiki notes it was reworded in a PC patch. [UNCERTAIN - may have changed after launch]

**Other rules:**
- **Codes.** The back-door code comes from an email on a nearby computer. [VERIFIED]
- **Double.** Killing the double before interrogating him fails the mission. [SINGLE SOURCE - FAN RU]

### 4. Spatial flow [VERIFIED macro chain - PRIMA + RYB]

**Macro route:** alley → hotel → back alley → wall and alley → zip line into the temple courtyard → sewer tunnel → far side of the temple → sewers → fish-market store → walkways over water → cafe alley → cafe.

- **Verticality.**
  - The hotel is a vertical puzzle: in high, down through floor holes, up through another hole, then up the stairs.
  - The zip line is a high-to-low entrance into an arena.
  - The sewers are an underground connector used twice.
- **Branches** (each [SINGLE SOURCE]):
  - the optional vent room;
  - zip line or pole into the temple;
  - two ways into the cafe, which merge at the target's locked door;
  - target captured in the cafe, or at the dock after a chase.
- **Loop.** The extraction truck sits beside the cafe, so a chase to the dock forces a backtrack.
- **Arenas.** The two anti-aircraft areas are open arenas around the temple, linked by a tunnel. Elsewhere the mission is a chain of tight interiors.

## C. Design reading (analysis)

1. **A war zone as a stealth space.** Ambient gunfire and noise make the city loud, so the threat comes from patrols and turrets, not silence. Sneaking through a battle is a strong fantasy, and the on-screen note tells players the gunfire isn't aimed at them.
2. **Twist-driven objective chain.** Capture → it's a double → find him → sabotage the vehicles (a detour) → recapture → carry out. Each update moves the pair into a new district: hotel, temple, market, cafe.
3. **Escort finale.** Carrying an unconscious target through doors makes one player the carrier and the other the door-opener and guard. It is a natural role split, and it raises the stakes at the end.
4. **Optional intel that prevents a hard fail is a trap.** Here, missing a computer leads to an unwinnable chase. A fairer version would give multiple cues or a recoverable chase.
5. **Shoulder-stand demolition** shows a contextual move used twice in an arena with a watcher. The repetition across two arenas teaches, then tests.
6. **The crossover radio beat** is cheap and memorable. A line of dialogue makes two campaigns feel like one world.
7. **The PS2/GC version** demonstrates a high/low route split that re-merges inside a building, and turret leapfrogging as the core rhythm of an area.
