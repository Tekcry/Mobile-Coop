## A. Xbox / PC

### 1. Setting and premise [VERIFIED - FAN + PRIMA]
- **Place.** An old missile bunker complex used as a secret bio-weapons lab near a North Korean port city.
- **How the lead was found.** An off-screen interrogator questioned the captured chemist. The chemist admitted passing bio-weapon research to an intelligence service, which then blackmailed him into smuggling lab equipment.
- **Task.** Get in, identify the weapons and bring back samples.
- **Story link.** In-game emails are addressed to the colonel who runs the bunker. He becomes the antagonist of the next two missions. [SINGLE SOURCE - FAN]
- **Character beat.** A lab chimpanzee named Java. A technician's email claims she can count, and mentions her blood is in the fridge. [VERIFIED - FAN email + several video titles/descriptions]

### 2. Objective chain [VERIFIED - FAN + PRIMA, same six primaries in the same order]
1. Get into the bunker down one of the missile silos. It is the only way in.
2. Recover the viral protocol from the main lab.
3. Take the matching chimpanzee blood serum.
4. Make an anti-virus in the cryogenic chamber and retrieve a sample.
5. Take a weaponised sample from a missile warhead.
6. Leave by inflatable boat from the missile-boat dock.

**Opportunity objective.** Recover a videotape of the chimp autopsy from the surveillance room. [VERIFIED]

**How the chain is built:**
- **Dependency.** Each primary feeds the next: the protocol leads to the serum, then the mix, then the anti-virus. The players are literally assembling a recipe across the map.
- **Codes.** Door codes come from interrogations and emails. Two emails explain the two-person mixing and the simultaneous wire cut before players reach those puzzles. [VERIFIED codes - FAN + Steam code list; emails SINGLE SOURCE - FAN]
- **Randomisation** [SINGLE SOURCE - FAN; randomness corroborated by NEO]:
  - The required blood type (A, B or O) is randomised per playthrough.
  - Each agent's colour sequence is randomised.
  - The sequence is 3, 4 or 5 inputs long on Normal, Hard and Expert.

### 4. Spatial flow [SINGLE SOURCE - PRIMA unless noted]
- **Surface to underground:** a twin-silo descent with two different shaft mechanics. [VERIFIED]
- **Lower level:** hall, then an overlook of the missile storage room (seen early, entered at the end), crawlspaces and vents, the Monkey Room, the laser corridor, the operating theatre (ledge, floor and a trapdoor to the camera room) and the main lab.
- **Upper level:** the mini lab stacked above the Monkey Room. The lab cluster spans two floors.
- **Back down:** the cryo stations at the tank floor, an elevator up, the storage and scanner door, the cryo upper gallery, a throw over the tank, a one-way door, an elevator down, the storage room, the mechanic room, the crane control, the sewers and the boat.
- **Hubs and revisits:** the operating theatre (hub), the cryotank (floor first, then the gallery above) and the missile storage room (overlook first, entry later).
- **Commitment point:** the one-way door after the tank throw stops backtracking.
- **Symmetric twin spaces:** stations A and B. [VERIFIED]

## C. Design reading (analysis)

1. **Recipe-chain objectives.** Protocol, serum, mixing, sample: each item unlocks the next. It gives a clear "what next" and justifies visiting every wing.
2. **Foreshadow and return.** The pair looks down into the final room in the first ten minutes. They even prepare it by killing its lights, and only enter it in the finale. It works as an earned landmark and pays off planning.
3. **Diegetic tutorials for sync puzzles.** Emails explain that mixing needs two people and that wires must be cut together. The game teaches through the fiction just before each test.
4. **Communication as the mechanic.** In the crane, one player has the controls and the other has the view. Neither can succeed alone, and the only channel is talk. It is the purest asymmetric-information beat in the campaign.
5. **Lethal precision puzzles split players.** The cryo mixing is memorable but also the main frustration, and on PC an input failure blocked progress entirely. Sync puzzles need forgiving timing, clear feedback and a fallback.
6. **One-way commitment after a throw** keeps the pair moving forward and prevents backtracking confusion.
7. **Real-world grounding.** Labs, cold storage, a cryo vessel, a surveillance room, a mechanic's room and a crane bay each have an obvious function. This plausibility makes the route readable.
