# 14 - Translation guide: original mechanic to original concept

This guide maps each original mechanic, move, gadget type, system and pattern to an **original, generic concept** for a new game. Each new concept gets a new name and is grounded in plausible real-world equipment and security practice.

The left column names the original (allowed in Phase 3). From Phase 4 on, only the right-hand names are used.

**How to read it:**
- **Setting-neutral** entries work in any setting.
- **Setting-dependent** entries give options. The choice is logged as a design decision (D-xx) in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, because the new game's setting is not known.
- **Why it works** states the underlying principle (see 13) so the concept can be re-expressed freely ("transform, don't trace").

---

## 1. Two-person moves

| Original | New generic name | Concept (original form) | Underlying principle | Real-world grounding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short Scale / Boost | **Leg-Up** | One player braces (cupped hands, knee or back) and lifts the partner to a ledge, window or wall top. The partner can also vault over | Two bodies reach heights one cannot (13 #1, #20) | Standard two-person climbing assist used by climbers and tactical teams |
| Human Ladder | **Ledge Relay** | The lifted player hangs from the ledge and lets the partner climb past them, so both arrive | Symmetric two-step: no one stranded (08 P4) | Partner pull-up |
| Tomoe Nage / Long Jump | **Partner Launch** | One player braces low and launches the partner over a low barrier or across a short gap. Anyone at the landing spot is knocked down | Spectacle + traversal + attack in one move (13 #4) | Exaggerated athletic assist. The new game must choose how grounded this is (D-07) |
| Dual Rappelling | **Belay Line** | One player anchors and pays out a line while the other descends or climbs. The anchor can swing the climber left or right | Operator/actor split with continuous control (08 P5) | Belay climbing; rope access for maintenance |
| Hang Over Teammate ("Hangover") | **Drop Line** | The anchor lowers the partner head-down through an opening to reach, hack, grab or take down. The suspended player can pull up to hide | Reach-in from above (08 P5) | Inverted rope work exists in rescue and film stunt practice; plausibility depends on tone (D-07) |
| Stand on Teammate's Shoulders | **Stack** | One player stands on the other's shoulders to reach a high device | Short-reach assist without a ledge | Common real two-person reach |
| Share View (sticky-camera feed) | **Feed Share** | One player's remote camera or drone feed can be watched by the partner | Overwatch-and-infiltrate (08 P8, P11) | Body-worn or deployable camera feeds shared on a team net |
| Heal / Revive (adrenaline) | **Pick-Up** (rescue window) | A downed player has a timed window during which the partner can bring them back | Failure becomes a rescue before a reload (13 #29) | Setting-dependent: medical stimulant, field dressing, "get up" assist (D-03) |
| Share Equipment | **Hand-Off** | Pass an item to the partner in reach | Kit complement (13 #8) | Natural |
| Synchronised retinal scan / wire cut / switches / code lock | **Two-Key Action** | Two devices or inputs that must be actuated within a tolerance window, usually placed apart | Split-then-sync rendezvous; dual control (13 #6, #7) | Two-person integrity, no-lone zones, dual-key vaults, split combinations, paired arming switches |
| Crane with voice directions | **Spotter-Operator** | One controls a machine without line of sight; the other sees the target and guides | Asymmetric information (13 #8; 08 P8) | Real crane operation uses a signal person with hand or radio signals |
| Captive forced to scanner | **Living Key** | A biometric or credential gate opened by escorting an authorised person to it. Two gates mean two captives | Capture + escort + sync in one beat (08 P7) | Biometric access control. Coercion level depends on tone (D-04) |
| Simultaneous takedown | **Double Drop** | Both players neutralise two linked guards at once so neither can raise an alarm | Coordination under time pressure | Paired guards on mutual watch |
| Automatic "partner waiting" voice lines | **Context Callouts** | Auto lines and pings for "ready", "done", "down", "up/down/left/right/stop" | Co-op without voice chat (13 #16) | Radio brevity codes |

## 2. Gadgets and tools

| Original | New generic name | Concept | Setting options (D-01) |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCP in co-op mode ("jammer"): hold to disable lights, cameras, lasers, turrets; drains; moving cancels | **Hold-Down Device** | A tool that suppresses an electronic or optical device *only while held still and aimed*, on a draining charge. It forces one player to cover while the other moves (hold-and-pass, 08 P2) | **Modern:** directed IR "dazzler" that blinds a camera's sensor, or a handheld RF suppressor for wireless sensors. **Near-future:** pulse emitter. **Historical:** hand mirror / lantern shutter to dazzle a watchman. **Fantasy:** a held ward. Grounding note: real cameras can be blinded by bright IR or glare (CCTV guidance lists glare and back-lighting as blind conditions) |
| Sticky camera (recon, noise lure, knockout gas) | **Throw-Cam** | A small adhesive or throwable camera for scouting; optional lure and incapacitant | Modern: adhesive micro-camera, throwable spherical camera, micro-drone. Lure: a speaker click. Incapacitant: tone-dependent (D-04) |
| Sticky shocker / ring airfoil (one-shot KO) | **Stun Round** / **Blunt Round** | Ranged non-lethal knockouts with limited count | Conducted-energy dart; bean-bag or blunt projectile; tranquiliser (period/tone) |
| Flash / smoke / gas grenades | **Flash**, **Screen**, **Sleep** charges | Area tools | Real distraction devices; smoke; incapacitant gas only if the tone allows (D-04) |
| Night / thermal / EM vision | **Sensor Modes**: low-light, heat, emitter | Reveal hidden information: wires, people, electronics | Modern: night-vision monocular, thermal imager, RF/EM detector. Historical: none, so replace with sound and lantern play (D-01) |
| EEV (remote hack/scan, disabled in co-op) | **Remote Probe** (optional) | Ranged scan or hack | Deliberately limited. Remote tools that are too powerful trivialise co-op (12 B16). **Decided (D-19): not included; all hacking is hands-on** |
| Optic cable | **Under-Door Scope** | Look under doors | Real snake/borescope cameras |
| Lockpick / break lock | **Pick** / **Force** | Quiet slow vs fast noisy entry | Real lock-picking vs forced entry |
| Keypad codes from emails / interrogation | **Intel Codes** | Codes found in the world | Real behaviour: sticky notes, emails, shoulder-surfing (observe a guard typing) |
| Retinal scanner | **Biometric Reader** | Iris, fingerprint, palm-vein or face reader | Real access control. Mantraps and dual authentication at high-value rooms |
| Laser grids | **Beam Field** | Intruder beams | Real active-IR beams are mostly perimeter devices, 0.6-1.8 m high, invisible. Visible lasers indoors need justification (a museum vault, a lab). Alternatives: PIR zones, floor pressure mats, door contacts |
| Wall mines | **Tamper Charges** | Booby traps | Setting-dependent; most civilian facilities have none (D-05) |
| Automated machine-gun turrets | **Automated Response** | A lethal automated defence | Unrealistic in most real facilities. Options: military or private compound only; or replace with remote-locking shutters, sirens and fog systems, a dispatched response team, or dogs (D-05) |
| Drone (PS2/GC UAV) | **Patrol Drone** | A mobile sensor | Real security drones exist (perimeter patrols) |
| Adrenaline syringe | **Revive Item** | Limited revives per mission | D-03 |
| Mission items (antidote, fuse, sample) | **Scarcity Items** | Single-use items that assign roles | A single dose, one key card, one hazmat suit |

## 3. Systems

| Original | New generic concept | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light meter | **Exposure** | Per-player exposure value that matches the visuals (fixes the original's mismatch) |
| Noise meter vs ambient marker | **Noise vs Ambient** | Show player noise against the local ambient floor; author masking zones (13 #33) |
| Alarm stages: armour → helmets → fortify, no abort | **Alert Tiers** | Example: Tier 0 routine → Tier 1 investigating → Tier 2 heightened (more patrols, protective gear) → Tier 3 lockdown (shutters, response team). Realistic mapping: a single sensor trip is ambiguous; cross-zone confirmation or video verification escalates (real-world notes §7). Hard abort only by explicit mission rule (D-02) |
| Body discovery by sight only | **Evidence Discovery** | Plus a realistic extra: a guard who misses a tour checkpoint triggers a supervisor check after a time window (guard-tour systems) |
| Guards notice doors, lights, missing colleagues | **Environmental Suspicion** | Keep it |
| Guards revive knocked-out colleagues | **Wake-Up** | Keep it; gives non-lethal play a cost |
| Voice audible to guards | **Proximity Voice** (optional) | D-06 |
| Success rating; perfect = no kills, no alarms, all objectives | **Clean Rating** | Rewards ghosting without forcing it |
| Story / Mission / Elite | **Campaign / Replay / No-Gear Challenge** | Elite translated as a challenge modifier (start with no lethal ammunition) |
| Normal / Hard / Expert | **Difficulty** | Decide what scales: perception, damage, timings (D-08) |
| Manual pause-menu saves that wait for the partner and reload only with the same teammate; no automatic checkpoints | **Encounter Checkpoints** | Fixes the top complaint (13 #29); allow rejoin with a different partner |
| Mission-specific loadouts (the no-kill finale gives both agents three of every non-lethal tool and no spare magazines) | **Rule-Signalling Loadout** | The kit states the rules (13 #35) |
| Controller's guidance lines (text-only in the downloads, which players missed) | **Voiced Guidance Channel** | Budget voice for every mission (13 #36) |
| Shared intel: one agent reads a computer, both mission devices update | **Shared Intel** | Keep it; pair with a shared floor plan once acquired (D-12) |
| Joint interrogation (both agents question one person to get a code) | **Two-Person Questioning** | Optional; depiction per D-04 |
| Mic feeds the guard sound meter; players lure guards by talking | **Proximity Voice** (lure use) | The most-loved systemic detail in community memory; D-06 |
| PS2/GC 3-alarm fail per sub-level | **Strict Mode** (optional) | Optional challenge only |
| Blue/red goggle lamps | **Partner Beacon** | Distinct colour or shape signature per player (13 #34) |
| Adaptive layered score | **State-Driven Score** | 15 §5 |

## 4. Objective types

The verbs are already generic (07). Use them as-is: retrieve data, search files, question, eavesdrop, plant a bug, capture alive, escort out, living key, eliminate (tone-dependent), sabotage, defuse, switch off machinery, tail, infiltrate by a given route, exfiltrate.

**Tone note.** "Interrogate", "eliminate" and "neck snap" depend on the game's violence stance (D-04). Non-violent alternatives:
- persuade or bluff;
- read a phone or ID badge;
- use leverage documents;
- tranquilise.

## 6. Setting-dependent translations (options; decisions logged)

| Concept | Modern day | Near future | Historical (e.g. early 20th century) | Fantasy / stylised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hold-Down Device | IR dazzler / RF suppressor | Pulse emitter | Mirror / lantern shutter vs watchmen | Ward / charm |
| Two-Key Action | Dual biometric, split combination, two-person arming | Same, plus quantum keys | Twin keyholes, two-handed vault wheel | Twin seals |
| Sensor Modes | NV / thermal / RF detector | Augmented overlays | None; rely on sound, stethoscope on safe | Sight spells |
| Living Key | Biometric gate | Neural or ID chip | Guard with keys, signet ring | Blood seal |
| Proximity Voice | Mic heard by guards | Same | Same | Same |
| Revive Item | Stimulant / field dressing | Med-injector | Smelling salts | Potion |
| Automated Response | Shutters, fogging, response team | Drones | Hounds, bell towers | Golems |

Decisions to be made by the user: setting/era (D-01), alarm rule (D-02), revive and failure model (D-03), lethal vs non-lethal stance (D-04), lethal automated defences (D-05), proximity voice (D-06), grounded vs heightened athletics (D-07), difficulty scaling (D-08). All are listed in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md and discussed in DESIGN_BIBLE.md.
