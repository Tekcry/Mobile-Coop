# Sector Ops - research

Reference research only: the structure of Conviction's "Deniable Ops" side mode. No names, maps, characters or text from
that game go into Night Shift (G5, G6).

Sources were thin: the main fan wiki, GameFAQs, GameSpot and achievement sites refused automated fetches, so most facts
come from search-result summaries of those pages, reviews and previews. Anything from a single forum post or a
pre-release build is marked "(unconfirmed)".

## 1. Maps and section flow

- Map pool, base game: six maps. Four reuse the co-op story maps (a Russian bathhouse block, an embassy, an underground
  complex under a city square, a military proving ground); two were made only for this mode (a lumber mill and a
  printing press). A seventh map (an agency HQ) came as an unlock code / weekly extra (unconfirmed).
- A DLC pack (May 2010): four more maps - city docks, a cemetery with an underground crypt, an old abandoned
  prison, a hidden research lab.
- Sections: each map is cut into separate zones ("areas"). Base maps have about 4-5 zones each (some players say 5-6);
  the DLC maps have about 3-4 (one review says 3 parts each; another says 5 areas with 3 used in Last Stand -
  conflicting, unconfirmed).
- A zone ends when its enemy count reaches zero. Only then does the route into the next zone open, and a weapon stash
  at the threshold restocks ammo and gadgets. Players report a checkpoint between completed zones.
- No source gives a fixed space pattern (courtyard, lobby, roof). What is documented:
  - Bathhouse map: four zones built around the bathhouse - two outdoor street zones with room to move, a connecting
    nightclub, and cramped steam rooms that open into two larger rooms which start lit but whose lights can be cut.
  - Docks map: starts inside a warehouse and reaches the open dock by the third part.
  - Cemetery map: graveyard above, crypt corridors below; the DLC maps are described as full of dark shadows, winding
    pipes, alternate staircases and crawlspaces.
  - Lumber mill: stacked timber rows, narrow wooden corridors, old machinery.
- Typical shape (inferred from the above): an exterior approach, then a transition building, then tighter interiors,
  ending in a larger final space. The order is fixed per map; the player chooses the entry point into each zone.
- A map has to be finished in one sitting: no mid-map save; maps can be played in any order.

## 2. Game types

- **Hunter** (solo or 2 players): clear every enemy in the current zone to unlock the next. An on-screen counter shows
  enemies left.
  - Zone size: about 10 enemies per zone, 8 in some zones; one player counted about 12 per zone on the cemetery map
    (unconfirmed).
  - Detection: an enemy who spots you calls in reinforcements, about as many again (10 becomes 20). Being "spotted"
    (the red warning) is forgiven if that enemy dies before he can call it in.
  - Mounted turrets: hacking their terminal disables them quietly; triggering them calls reinforcements.
  - A pre-release preview said the count can rise only once per level, so later detections add nothing
    (unconfirmed for the final game).
  - Patrols and spawn spots vary between runs.
  - Death: restarts the current zone from its checkpoint; the no-detection achievement does not count a retried run.
- **Infiltration** (solo or 2 players; an unlock, not available at first): the same zone-clearing goal, but zero
  tolerance.
  - Anything that would bring reinforcements in Hunter ends the mission: a guard seeing you, a camera seeing you or a
    body, an alarm, any loud noise - even after the zone is already cleared.
  - Adds security: cameras and laser tripwires (lasers show in the sonar goggles). The wiki says shooting a camera
    does not fail you outright but can draw guards (conflicting reports).
  - Players advise watching patrols for several minutes before moving and carrying only silent weapons.
  - Whether a fail restarts the zone or the whole map is not documented (unconfirmed).
- **Last Stand** (solo or 2 players): defend a device (an EMP generator) against 20 waves of growing numbers, armour
  and weapons. Start at wave 1, 5, 10 or 15. The device's health partly refills between waves (up to the 50% or 25%
  step it fell below). Ends when the device is destroyed or both players are down. Supply crates restock between
  waves.
- **Face-Off** (2 players only, competitive): two rival spies on one map with AI guards. Points for guard kills, a
  bonus for killing the rival, a penalty for dying. The only mode where players can kill each other.
- Difficulty: Rookie, Normal, Realistic, chosen before each run.

## 3. Guards, patrols, security

- About 8-12 guards per zone (see Hunter). Guards are armed mercenaries / soldiers; later Last Stand waves bring
  heavier armour. No per-zone type lists were found.
- Patrol routes and starting spots are partly random each run, so fixed walkthroughs are unreliable.
- Guards investigate found bodies with flashlights; cameras that see a body trigger the response.
- Security devices: cameras, laser tripwires, mounted turrets with hack terminals. Infiltration leans on them most.
- Light: shot-out lights are a standard tool; one reviewer noted guards locate the shooter very precisely when a light
  is shot.
- Realistic difficulty is the bar for the stealth achievements (clear a Hunter map undetected on Realistic).

## 4. Co-op specifics

- Hunter, Infiltration and Last Stand all work solo or with a partner; maps were not rescaled for one player
  (unconfirmed).
- Downed partner (documented for the co-op story; assumed the same here, unconfirmed): about 60 s to bleed out; the
  downed player can lie still or sit up with the pistol (which draws fire); a downed player can shoot the guard who has
  grabbed his partner so the partner can then revive him.
- Both players down: back to the last checkpoint (zone start).
- Checkpoints are shared; a reload reloads both players. One report says a reload can show the partner as having left
  (a bug, unconfirmed).
- Whether both players must stand at the zone exit is not documented; zones unlock on enemy count, not on position.

## 5. What made sections feel different

- Open outdoor zones (room to flank, multiple entries) against cramped interiors (steam rooms, crypt corridors,
  cell blocks).
- Lighting as a zone feature: zones that start lit and can be darkened by cutting lights, against zones that are dark
  by default.
- Verticality and alternate routes: staircases, pipes, crawlspaces, ledges (pulling a guard over a ledge).
- Security mix: turret and camera zones play differently from plain patrol zones.
- Setting change inside one map (warehouse to open dock, graveyard to crypt) gave each zone its own read.

## Chaos Theory co-op story missions (contrast)

- Seven co-op missions on Xbox / PC: training, four story missions on disc, two free downloads. Each is one continuous
  level, 20-35 min for a careful stealth run.
- No automatic checkpoints: pause-menu save and load that both players share.
- Alarms escalate in four stages (armour, then helmets, then permanent high alert with fortified posts). Reaching the
  top stage does not fail the mission; it cancels optional objectives. One mission aborts on a general alarm; a few
  missions fail on any detection or on a kill.
- The "3 alarms = fail" rule belongs to older games and to the cut-down console co-op versions (PS2 / GameCube, 3
  alarms fail a sub-level), not to the Xbox / PC reference.
- Goals are objectives (data, captives, bombs), with optional and hidden ones; lethal force costs score, not the
  mission. Downed partner: about 30 s for a revive syringe, one each per mission.
- Pacing: about one primary objective per 5-10 minutes, a co-op set piece every 5-8 minutes.

## What this means for a Chaos Theory paced version

- Keep the zone skeleton: a map cut into 3-5 sectors, each sealed until its goal is met, with a resupply point and a
  shared checkpoint at each threshold.
- Sector goals are objectives (hack, retrieve, plant, escort, reach) rather than a body count; clearing guards is one
  optional way to make a goal easier.
- Replace "detected = reinforcements" with CT's four alarm stages: each stage hardens the guards (armour, helmets, then
  fortified posts) and stays for the rest of the map.
- No instant fail on detection by default; a sector may carry one hard rule (no alarm, no kill) and says so in its
  briefing.
- Reward ghosting in the score: no alarms, no bodies found, no kills, never identified, optional objectives done.
- Light first: every sector has a light plan (lamps to cut, switches, dark routes) and at least one sector that starts
  lit and must be darkened.
- Vary sector shape on purpose: an open exterior approach, a tight interior, a vertical sector, a security-heavy
  sector with cameras and lasers, a larger final space.
- Slower pacing: fewer guards per sector than Hunter's 10 (about 4-8), longer patrol loops, room to watch before moving.
- Each sector has at least one two-player beat (boost, paired switch, one jams a camera while the other moves).
- Both players must stand in the exit zone to open the next sector (our rule; neither reference documents it).
- Downed partner is a rescue task before it is a reload: revive window, then both down = sector checkpoint.
- Patrols vary a little between runs so a replay is not a memorised route.
