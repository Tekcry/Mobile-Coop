## 1. Core identity

- Elite covert infiltrators: alone or in small teams, at night, inside places they should not be.
- Heavily inspired by Splinter Cell, above all Chaos Theory. Quiet professionals, grounded realism, near future but
  believable, nothing fantastical. Wren reads as a stealth operative, not a soldier: fitted, slim and expensive
  (D22, D23; Section 9).
- Every item has a job. Kit with no purpose does not exist. Anything that would rattle, shine or snag is taped,
  tucked, matted or removed.
- Mood: dark, tactical, cold, controlled. Dangerous because they look competent, not because they look aggressive.
- Original, always:
  - no copy of the reference hero's suit or any reference-game character;
  - no three-lens goggle cluster with three green lights;
  - no logos of the reference game's organisations, and no flag, unit or brand logos;
  - no recognisable trademarked equipment shapes.
- The inspiration is the tone, the function and the darkness, never a specific design.

## 2. Body and silhouette

- Lean, athletic, trained: climbers and swimmers, not bodybuilders. Defined shoulders and forearms, narrow waist,
  strong legs. Functional, not bulky. (Waist 0.93 of SPEC A4, D13.)
- Wren 1.68 m, Moth 1.78 m. Builds, roles and differences come from SPEC 4.4 and the joint export.
- Low profile: kit sits tight. Nothing sticks out past the shoulders or hips except what must (holster, pack).
- The silhouette reads instantly against a rim light: head with optic, shoulder line, harness straps, thigh holster,
  compact pack low on the back. (Wren: head with optic, the plate's shoulder line and slim straps, the thigh holster
  and sleeves, the narrow belt; the game's long gun on the back mount. Section 9.)
- Nothing loose or flowing: no capes, coats, scarves, dangling straps or loose cords. Every strap end is rolled,
  banded or tucked.

## 4. Colour

- Near-black, deep charcoal, dark slate, dark olive. No pure black: albedo stays above about 0.02-0.03 linear so
  forms still read under a rim light.
- Value contrast comes from material, not colour: knit, ripstop, Cordura, leather, rubber and polymer are each
  distinguishable in near-darkness by texture and sheen alone.
- Light accents only where real kit has them: the IR strobe, status LEDs. Muted and small. No glowing suit lines, no
  neon, no decorative light.
- Exception (Michael, D13): the goggle lenses glow clearly in the dark, as SPEC 2's signature (Wren green, Moth amber).
- No bright colours, no camouflage, no flags or patches. Hook-and-loop identity panels stay blank. (Wren: no
  hook-and-loop at all, Section 9.)
- Wren's metal is gunmetal: dark anodised grey, satin-matte, the only metal on her. Her composite plates are a hair
  lighter than the suit so the plate edges catch a rim light.

## 5. Materials and wear

- Fabrics: matte nylon ripstop with a visible fine grid, soft knit torso, heavy Cordura reinforcements, stretch panels
  at the joints. Roughness about 0.7-0.95; only a faint fibre sheen at grazing angles.
- Hard parts: matte polymer buckles and clips, matte coated metal, rubberised edges. No chrome, no gloss.
- Construction holds up at close range: double-stitched seams, bar tacks at stress points, taped seam edges, zip
  garages, folded hems, hook-and-loop with a visible loop texture. (Wren: bonded and taped seams, no visible
  stitching on the suit, no hook-and-loop; Section 9.)
- Wear is subtle and earned: light dust at knees, elbows and boot toes; slight abrasion and fading on high-wear edges;
  a little grime in seams. Maintained professionals, not apocalypse survivors.

## 6. The combat shirt

Moth (until step 12). Wren's combat shirt is replaced by the one-piece suit (D22-D24, Section 9).

- Torso: breathable knit, close fit, worn under armour, slight stretch at the sides.
- Sleeves: ripstop, raglan shoulder seams, articulated pre-bent elbows, gusseted underarms (arms reach overhead
  without lifting the hem).
- Collar: quarter-zip mock neck, snug to the neck, zip garage at the top.
- Upper arms: slanted pockets with flap closures and a blank hook-and-loop panel on each.
- Elbows: internal pad pockets with reinforced outer panels in heavier fabric.
- Cuffs: snug, with a hook-and-loop tab so they seal over the gloves.
- Colours: charcoal knit torso, near-black ripstop sleeves, slightly darker reinforcement panels.

## 7. How they read in the game

- In darkness: a silhouette. The rim light catches the shoulder line, the arms, the optic and kit edges; the body
  falls to near-black.
- Under a lamp or torch the material detail appears: seams, the fabric grid, wear, knit against ripstop.
- In a still frame they belong to a serious covert unit and could vanish into the shadow behind them.

## 8. Avoid

- Sci-fi power armour, bulky exosuits, cyberpunk, glowing suit lines, superhero styling.
- Mall-ninja gear: skulls, spikes, oversized knives, excess pouches, decorative straps.
- Parade or infantry looks: covered helmets with scrim, heavy pouch-laden carriers, camouflage.
- Fantasy and stylised cartoon proportions.
- Against Wren's premium look: gloss, chrome, logos, visible fasteners that click or rattle, lumpy or improvised kit,
  mismatched corner radii, more than one metal finish.

### 9.1 The look standard
- **Premium one-piece suit.** A wetsuit-fitted one-piece in a bonded stretch soft-shell: smooth tension over the body,
  no slack folds, no bagging. It reads as an expensive, engineered garment, not paint on skin and not a costume.
- **Bonded seams.** Panel seams where a real suit has panels (yoke, side panel, sleeve, knee, seat), sealed with a
  narrow tonal tape (about 6-12 mm wide, under 1 mm proud). No visible stitching on the suit. Panel lines follow the
  body's structure and meet without kinks.
- **Micro-texture.** The main shell carries a fine tonal diamond micro-emboss (about 1.6 mm) that reads only at
  0.3-1 m; from 3 m the suit is one matte form.
- **Vents.** Laser-perforated panels (about 1.4 mm holes on a 3.2 mm pitch, the darker mesh behind) at the underarm
  side panels and the backs of the knees.
- **Grip, abrasion and stretch zones.** Rubberised micro-pebble grip panels on the forearms and knees (crawling,
  vents, wall contact). Abrasion panels in a denser weave at the shoulder yokes, elbows and seat. Stretch knit at the
  inner elbows and rear shoulders.
- **Closures.** A diagonal chest zip (from the left of the collar down to the right ribs) with a zip garage at the
  collar; thumb loops under the glove cuffs; a close mock neck the hood skirt laps over; bonded ankle cuffs inside the
  boot collars.
- **Composite plates.** A contoured chest plate with a neck scoop and three overlapping rib segments, and a slim back
  plate ending at the ribs. Matte resin with a faint fibre texture, chamfered edges with a rubber trim, every corner
  from one radius family. Moulded to the body: they follow the torso, they do not stand off it like a slab.
- **Gunmetal magnetic hardware.** Every closure is a flush gunmetal magnetic quick-release: two halves with a split
  line on a rubber-edged base. One metal finish everywhere.
- **Silent.** No hook-and-loop. Nothing that rattles, clicks, swings or snags: no loose metal, no dangling tabs, no
  open loops; strap ends are bonded or held flush. Retention is shock cord with pull tabs, magnetic tabs or friction.
- **Slimline storage mapped to the game loadout.** Every pouch holds something the game gives Wren (table below), at
  the hand that uses it, and stands no more than about 2.5 cm off the body (magazine pouches up to about 4 cm).
- **Not modelled (D24).** No rifle and no pistol: the game's weapon models attach in game. The holster is empty,
  shaped for the game's sidearm; the back plate carries the weapon mount point the game's back carry slots use (A36).

## Render check (every operator render, with SPEC 1's checklist)

1. Does the silhouette read in near-darkness?
2. Does the fabric stay matte?
3. Do knit and ripstop read as different materials without colour? (Wren: do the suit's shell, stretch, abrasion,
   grip and vent zones, the composite, the rubber trim and the gunmetal read as different materials without colour?)
4. Do the folds look sewn, not sculpted? (Wren: does the suit read as a bonded, fitted garment - smooth tension,
   taped seams, no stitching - and not as paint on the skin?)
5. Does anything break the covert-operative feel?
6. Wren: does it look expensive (D23)? Clean panel lines, one corner-radius family, one metal finish, nothing lumpy,
   improvised or mismatched.
7. Wren: is it silent and snag-free? No hook-and-loop, no loose metal or loops, nothing standing proud that would
   catch in a vent or on a rope.
