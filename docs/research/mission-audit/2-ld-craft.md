# Night Shift mission audit 2/3 - How real level designers do it
Researched 2026-10-24. Each claim has a URL. Quotes are verbatim and under 15 words. "Inference" marks our own reading, not a source.

## 1. Multi-path sections without open-world sprawl
- **Every obstacle gets 2+ solutions, each planned on purpose.** Deus Ex postmortem: "more than one way to get past a game obstacle. Always." Plan a skill path, an action path and a character-interaction (social) path. Solutions that "fall out" of the simulation happened less often than hoped, so routes were built by hand. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-ion-storm-s-i-deus-ex-i-
- Deus Ex HR kept the same rule: "multi-path, multi-solution for practically every challenge", with some options more obvious than others. https://www.gamestar.de/artikel/deus-ex-human-revolution-viele-loesungswege-spielen-eine-wichtige-rolle,2320282.html
- **Loops, not tubes.** "Tunnels that go direct from Point A to Point B are bad." "Dead ends should be avoided unless tactically significant." (Deus Ex postmortem, above). Level Design Book: a loopback layout keeps linear control with less player indecision, but loopbacks that are too convenient feel contrived. https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/typology
- **Converge on choke points.** Reviewers of SC Chaos Theory describe several intertwining routes that converge on the same endpoint, not open labyrinths. Objectives could also be done out of order. https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/36141-splinter-cell-chaos-theory-review ; https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/splinter-cell-chaos-theory/article1117156
- **"Wide-linear" (Naughty Dog).** A linear macro sequence with wide spaces inside it. Lost Legacy's Western Ghats has three tasks in any order, and it "plays out the same no matter which Ghats objective you go to first" (James Cooper). https://cogconnected.com/feature/interview-lost-legacy-developers/
- **Onion layout (Payday 2 Murky Station, Jason Mojica).** Concentric rings from an outer overpass in to the main objective. A 10-15 min heist is split into sub-goals of about 2 min each. Routes run under trains and through vents. https://80.lv/articles/designing-highly-replayable-stealth-levels-for-payday-2/
- **Multi-day/phase instead of huge maps.** Goldfarb: multi-day heists make "long, complex heists without having to build five hour maps." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payday_2
- **Swiss cheese density.** Sapienza and the Clockwork Mansion are both described as Swiss cheese: dense, layered, little backtracking, hidden routes behind walls and under floors. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_(Hitman) ; https://gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-deep-dive-i-dishonored-2-s-i-clockwork-mansion
- **Hub-and-spoke works if the hub changes.** A hub that does not update on each visit feels like backtracking. Gating can open shortcuts gradually. https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/typology
- **Avoid real-building literalism.** "Hotels and office buildings aren't great game spaces." Favour interconnected, multi-level spaces (Deus Ex postmortem, above).
- **Route types for us:** vents and pipes are an explicit Blacklist route class ("Hidden Paths") rewarded by its scoring. https://primagames.com/news/splinter-cell-blacklist-playstyle-campaign-scoring . Social routes: Hitman disguises; Dishonored's Lady Boyle party, where three masked suspects have to be told apart (PC Gamer making-of, which we could not fetch in full). https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-making-of-dishonoreds-greatest-mission-lady-boyles-last-party/
- **Too many routes backfires.** Prey reviewers praised its approach variety but also called Talos I convoluted when you lack the right skill. https://www.pcworld.com/article/3194987/prey-pc-review-system-shocks-spirit-lives-in-this-mammoth-intertwined-space-station.html ; https://www.powerpyx.com/prey-ps4-review/

## 2. Guidance without markers or yellow paint
- **Mental-map architecture.** Arkane's Clockwork Mansion goal was to let the player "construct a mental picture of the house". They avoided "long and tight passages" and kept backstage routes "visually connected to the big areas". Rooms have "specific functions that the player could relate to", which supports play "without markers". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-deep-dive-i-dishonored-2-s-i-clockwork-mansion
- **Connectivity removes the need for markers.** Elverdam on Sapienza: "these connections mean that you will never get lost". Hiding where the level starts and stops keeps exploration organic. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/from-coastal-town-to-sapienza-designing-a-i-hitman-i-level
- **Show the goal first.** "Always show the goal." Every destination should be reachable by more than one route (Deus Ex postmortem, above).
- **Everything guides.** The Level Design Book says "everything in the game is a wayfinding aid". It also says "Players don't look upwards unless something draws their eye", which matters for ledge and rappel routes. Its tools are landmarks (HL2 Citadel), weenies, lit entrances and exits, colour contrast, framed windows, ground lines (planks, rails), and NPCs or wildlife moving toward the goal. https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/wayfinding
- **Light as path.** Robert Yang (GDC 2018 LD workshop with Naughty Dog's David Shaver): light the exits and the critical path, "Don't light everything the same", and "try to keep your lighting language consistent". Putting something in shadow is a statement about how much it matters. https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2018/03/gdc-2018-how-to-light-level-slides-and.html
- **Naughty Dog's toolkit:** a clear path, framing, a light source against dark surroundings, a single contrasting colour, and motion such as birds flying toward the exit. https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-12213 ; https://amara.org/videos/xwcncEJkgSYU/en/2806945
- **Stealth twist (inference):** in a light/shadow game, lit areas mean danger and objective, and dark areas mean the route. A lit, guarded doorway marks the front door. A dark pipe beside it is the invitation. This only works if lighting stays readable. Clint Hocking says baked lighting was "very clean and readable and very understandable for the player", while modern diffuse lighting blurs what is safe. https://frvr.com/blog/news/splinter-cell-designer-says-one-of-the-difficulties-with-modern-stealth-games-is-realistic-lighting/
- **Consistent affordance language.** Payday 2 used one consistent visual vocabulary (caution tape, red lights) for areas you can explore or hide in, and gave each key and vault prefab its own audio and visuals so they read at a distance. https://80.lv/articles/designing-highly-replayable-stealth-levels-for-payday-2/ . Arkane's Steve Lee (GDC 2017) builds holistic LD on affordances and player intentionality. https://gdcvault.com/play/1024301/Level-Design-Workshop-An-Approach
- **Overheard dialogue as guidance.** Hitman Opportunities start when 47 overhears a conversation. The UI hints can be cut down or turned off, so the conversation alone carries the lead. https://www.empireonline.com/gaming/reviews/hitman-2015-review/ . Hitman 3 rewards players who read mischief from NPC chatter. https://videogameschronicle.com/review/hitman-3 . Thief built life through chatter, with no player-NPC dialogue. https://videohighlight.com/v/i_Okvwf-95k
- **When the sandbox is too big, add a guidance layer.** IO's user research found Paris's scale "incomprehensible". They added a tutorial and a linear guidance layer and kept the sandbox. https://gdcvault.com/play/1023849/Level-Design-in-HITMAN-Guiding
- **Teach, then test.** Nintendo's four steps (kishotenketsu): players first "learn how to use that gameplay mechanic", then a harder use, a twist, and a mastery beat (Hayashida). https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design
- **Scouting vantage points.** Murky Station's spawn overpass shows the whole yard, and snipers mark guards for the team below (80.lv, above). MGS V lets players circle, or go over or under, and the team "went out of our way not to guide you". https://www.metalgearinformer.com/?p=8375

## 3. Mission structure
- **Intel, then exploitation.** Hitman 3 leans into gathering intel from the world, with branching outcomes. It also cuts a linear first-run beat (Chongqing) on replay. https://videogameschronicle.com/review/hitman-3
- **Planning choice picks the prep.** In GTA V's Jewel Store, the Smart or Loud choice decides the prep missions, gear and crew role. https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Casing_the_Jewel_Store
- **Sub-goals of about 2 min, plus escalation audio.** Murky Station adds a music layer per completed sub-objective, and completion times drop by a third to a half as players learn (80.lv, above).
- **Phase rules inside one mission.** A Blacklist story co-op mission (a missile plant) starts undetected and non-lethal, and lethal force opens later. https://digitaltrends.com/?p=439902
- **Failure branches the mission instead of ending it.** In Payday 2, a loud day can add an escape level. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payday_2
- **Optional objectives drive replay.** SC Chaos Theory reviews credited optional objectives and loose structure. https://gamespot.com/videos/tom-clancys-splinter-cell-chaos-theory-video-revie/2300-6121211/
- **Fewer, richer threads beat many thin ones.** Hitman 3 has fewer Mission Stories, but deeper ones (VGC, above).
- **Pacing curve.** The L4D Director plans attacks when things go quiet and backs off after long intensity. https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2007/04/10/11866.html

## 4. Stealth-specific
- **Readable state.** Thief's early stealth gave "confused signals". The light gem fixed it. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-the-original-i-thief-s-i-revolutionary-stealth-system . Mark of the Ninja's rules: "light and darkness are totally binary"; transparent AI with 3 awareness states, so "the player will immediately understand what set it off"; "a checkpoint between every meaningful encounter"; keep spaces mentally mappable. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-mark-of-the-ninja-i-s-five-stealth-design-rules
- **Recoverable failure over instant fail.** Players criticised SC Conviction's instant fail on any detection or found body. https://www.giantbomb.com/user-articles/id/36296/where-splinter-cell-conviction-fails-and-succeeds . Payday 3 moved to soft fails: "You can make a mistake and not go loud immediately" (Storlokken). https://gamingbolt.com/payday-3-includes-soft-fails-for-stealth-more-uses-for-civilians
- **Patrol tuning.** Short patrol loops with more points cut worst-case guard stacking from about 8 to about 4. Chain reactions, where a body is found by the next guard, are managed deliberately. Waits are kept short. Payday 2 raises the alarm after 4 guard kills (80.lv, above).
- **Ratings make stealth worth it.** Hitman 2's Silent Assassin rating was added because the first game gave no reason to play stealthily. https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/hitman-2-silent-assassin-was-the-birth-of-everything-that-makes-the-series-great . Blacklist pays Ghost (stealth, non-lethal) most, Panther (stealth, lethal) next and Assault least. One tool, the whistle, serves all three. https://primagames.com/news/splinter-cell-blacklist-playstyle-campaign-scoring ; https://gamingbolt.com/splinter-cell-blacklist-interview-play-styles-wii-u-version-jade-raymonds-influence-and-more
- **Pacifist must be fully supported.** Arkane said non-lethal began "almost" as an easter egg in Dishonored 1. Dishonored 2 added non-lethal tools rather than changing the chaos system. https://gamebanshee.com/xx9 ; https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dishonored-2-preview/2
- **Playtest metrics.** Bungie published death heatmaps. Microsoft's lab tracked routes and engagement prompts to find spots where players got lost or died repeatedly. https://gamesradar.com/where-not-to-stand-in-halo-3 ; https://blog.kartones.net/post/wired-article-on-halo-3/

## 5. Co-op
- **2-player gates.** SC Chaos Theory had one co-op puzzle per area (for example, a synchronised bomb defuse). Throws and human ladders worked only at specific spots, which reviewers flagged as limiting. https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tom-clancys-splinter-cell-chaos-theory-first-look/1100-6116458 ; https://gamespot.com/videos/tom-clancys-splinter-cell-chaos-theory-video-revie/2300-6121211/ . Hazelight builds co-op-only with no drop-in: "You have to play with someone." https://www.theringer.com/2021/04/22/video-games/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming . Portal 2 made death recoverable because of the partner. https://www.pcgamer.com/au/interview-valve-on-their-insane-portal-2-ideas
- **Split information between players.** In GTFO one player reads a code and another enters it. There is little direction, so the team scouts together. https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/rethinking-the-co-op-shooter-with-gtfo
- **Parallel work.** Payday 3: "players can do a lot of different things in parallel" and "there is not one linear path". Payday 2's non-linear sub-objectives let teams split (gamingbolt and 80.lv, above). Ready or Not splits a squad into Red and Blue elements for simultaneous breaches. https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/16245/Ready-or-Not/
- **Rule-constrained missions with 3-4 players (Michael's correction):**
  - *Shared hard rule:* Blacklist's strict no-alarm intel missions "need to be completed without detection" (Zack Cooper). In co-op, either player being detected fails both. https://blog.playstation.com/2013/05/23/splinter-cell-blacklist-co-op-2-of-4-mission-types-revealed/ ; https://digitaltrends.com/?p=439902
  - *Shared budget:* Payday 2's alarm triggers on a team-wide 4-kill threshold (80.lv, above). Community reports say pagers are a pool of 4 shared by the whole team (forum source, unverified). https://steamcommunity.com/app/218620/discussions/8/527273452871145511
  - *Graded instead of binary:* Ready or Not grades rules of engagement after the mission, with penalties that scale with severity. Barricaded Suspects requires arrests for a high score. https://readyornot.wiki.gg/wiki/Ready_or_Not
  - *Soft fail per player:* Payday 3 escorts a caught player out of a private area instead of raising the alarm (gamingbolt, above).
  - *Critical NPC:* in Payday 3, killing a Lead Guard ends stealth. https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/PAYDAY3
  - *Score credit:* Blacklist co-op scoring reflects both partners' styles, so a loud partner costs the stealth player (digitaltrends, above).
  - *Inference:* with 4 players and 2 parallel teams, one player's mistake fails 3 others, so prefer graded or escalating rules (search, then lockdown, then fail) over one-strike fails. Exceptions are short, clearly flagged ghost phases. Give each pair its own rule-bearing sub-objective, such as an interrogation target or a no-kill zone, so responsibility is local.
- **Solo scaling.** Blacklist's story co-op missions "have to be played with a partner". https://blog.playstation.com/2013/05/23/splinter-cell-blacklist-co-op-2-of-4-mission-types-revealed/ . *Inference:* every co-op gate needs a solo bypass (a longer route or a tool), or the mission is tagged co-op-only.

## 6. Process
- **Beat chart:** one page with a row per location: mechanics, story, what the player learns, time, content and audio. "do not overload player with new information". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/beat-chart-game-designer-s-best-friend
- **Bubble diagram, then layout, then blockout.** Mark the critical path, desire lines and metrics, and verify in blockout. https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/flow
- **IO's order:** location, then characters. Robert Knox is a "dweller" who stays in one place (Hitman 2 Miami, GMTK). https://amara.org/subtitles/grmg8UPEXjYO/en/1/download/The%20Making%20of%20Hitman%202s%20Miami%20Level%20%20Game%20Makers%20Toolkit.en.txt . Sapienza's rough version was built in about two weeks. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_(Hitman)
- **Cheap tests before scripting.** Role-play objectives with the team. Designers test their own randomisation because QA cannot cover every combination (Mojica, 80.lv above). Deus Ex: "Get this mission working as early as possible." General solutions beat special-case scripting (postmortem, above).
- **Playtest loop:** heatmaps of deaths and detections, route traces, and lost-player hotspots (Halo 3, above). Completion time should fall as players learn (Mojica).

## Rules we should adopt (checkable by a map spec or validator)
1. Every section has at least 2 routes from entry to exit, and at least 3 in key sections: one loud or lit, one dark (vent, ledge, pipe), one social or tool route (keycard, interrogation, hack, disguise).
2. No dead end unless it holds a pickup, intel, a hide spot or a vantage. Validator: every leaf node in the route graph is tagged.
3. Routes reconnect at a choke node at least once per section. No branch runs to mission end without rejoining.
4. The main objective is visible (a landmark, lit window or tower) from at least one point in each section before the player reaches it.
5. The critical-path exit of each section is lit with a distinct, consistent light colour or source type. The dark alternate route is visible from the lit route's approach.
6. Lighting language is consistent: one fixed meaning per light type (spotlight = patrolled, warm lamp = objective or social area, dark = safe). The validator checks tags against a palette.
7. Each section has at least one scouting vantage that shows at least 50% of the guards' patrol paths.
8. Each objective has at least one diegetic lead: an overheard conversation, a document or map, or an interrogation. No objective relies on UI alone.
9. Sub-objectives take about 2-5 minutes each. Missions run 3-6 steps (target 10-30 min), shown on a beat chart. Each mission introduces at most one new mechanic.
10. Patrols are short loops: no more than 4 guards' cones overlap any cell. Every patrol cycle shows a guard's back to the dark route within 20 s.
11. A full ghost run (no detection, no kills) and a pacifist run are proven possible by a playtest or path-check before shipping.
12. Detection escalates in steps (suspicious, search, alert, lockdown) before any fail. One-strike fail is allowed only in short, flagged "ghost phase" segments with a checkpoint at the start.
13. 2-player campaign: at least 1 co-op gate per section (boost, ladder, rappel, team door), each with a solo bypass or a co-op-only tag.
14. 4-player Sector Ops: each mission has at least 2 parallel sub-objectives on separate routes, so teams can split 2+2. Mission rules (ghost, no kills, interrogate) are scored per team, and one player's breach escalates the alarm before it fails everyone.
15. Telemetry per section: detections, deaths, time and route taken. Flag any section where one route gets over 80% of runs, or median time is over 2x the beat-chart target.
