## 1. Chapter-by-chapter co-op catalogue

### 1.0 What carries over in both games
- **Only movement carries over** between chapters: run, double jump, dash, wall jump, rail grind, grapple swing. Every
  other ability is chapter-local. Fares: otherwise "new mechanics every time". https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=375927 ;
  move list: https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/03/24/it-takes-two-review/
- SF does the same: both writers share an identical base moveset (double jump, sprint, dash / air dash, grapple), so
  neither starts weaker. https://burlybirdmedia.com/archive/shoutoutsplitfictioncoop ; https://hardcoregamer.com/review-split-fiction/
- Each SF main level hands each player two new abilities and removes them at the level's end.
  https://hardcoregamer.com/review-split-fiction/
- Fares on the craft problem: "The hardest thing is the duration of the mechanic."
  https://80.lv/articles/it-takes-two-creator-shared-how-filmmaking-helped-the-game-mechanics
- Enemies are built per mechanic and cannot be reused across sections, so each level needs near-fresh design.
  https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=375927

## 2. Teaching

- **Teach by doing, then escalate inside the level.** Kotaku: each level introduces a mechanic, then shows new ways to use
  it. https://kotaku.com/split-fiction-review-hazelight-coop-game-it-takes-two-1851767470 . Hazelight frames each level as
  teaching "a new game in an hour" (GMTK, via prior file) https://amara.org/subtitles/tUIr67mipkE6/en/1/download/It%20Takes%20Two.en.vtt
- **A worked escalation (Cuckoo Clock)** [verify, walkthrough site]: (1) rewind alone on an obvious broken statue and wall;
  (2) first combined use: one opens a timed door, the other teleports through; (3) rewind as a *hold* while the partner
  crosses; (4) clone as a landing anchor; (5) rewind drives the partner's movement; (6) clone covers two buttons while
  the other holds a third; (7) sustained two-ability combinations at a drawbridge.
  https://walkthroughs.games/lorebase/it-takes-two/missions/gates-of-time . Pattern [Inference]: solo practice on a
  safe target, one handover, holds, then both abilities chained under time pressure.
- **A tutorial landmark, then variations:** in the Snow Globe village the first of four bell towers is the tutorial; the
  other three each need a different solution so nothing repeats; each goal has a light pole and floating UI above it.
  https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1768861/FULLTEXT01.pdf
- **Tutorial length is a pressure point.** Hazelight spends "a lot" of time shortening tutorials; long ones kill momentum.
  https://videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/split-fiction-game-interview . Most SF mechanics are borrowed from
  familiar games, which lets it skip long tutorials. https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0787/15/1/2 (search summary)
- **Prompts:** SF puts a button icon and a short title above the character; each writer has a colour (purple, green) but
  the icon means colour is not the only cue. https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 . Hazelight says no ITT gameplay relies on
  reading colour alone (search summary of a developer quote) https://www.ea.com/able/resources/it-takes-two/ps5 [verify]
- **Stopping one player solving both halves:**
  - Ability-locked interactions: only the hammer swings from nails, only the match ignites sap. https://hone.gg/blog/it-takes-two-chapters-list/
  - Colour-locked targets: in one SF chapter each blaster only hurts its own colour. https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=164561
  - Fares: "most of the time it's my mechanic and your mechanic"; identical shared actions are avoided.
    https://videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/split-fiction-game-interview
  - Ambiguous shared prompts made both players act on both targets; each role is taught separately (GMTK, prior file).
  - Counter-example: the clone lets one player cover two buttons alone, which is fine because it is still that player's
    own ability. https://walkthroughs.games/lorebase/it-takes-two/missions/gates-of-time
  - SF finale: an early cable-cutting puzzle was accidentally solvable by one player; later versions required both to
    act at once. https://www.invisiblefriends.net/gdc-2026-a-personal-account/
- **The cost of always teaching:** critics felt every world was "the introduction phase to a new game", which stops
  momentum. https://www.gamegrin.com/articles/why-i-think-it-takes-two-is-overrated/ ; ITT puzzles often spelled out by
  the characters' dialogue (same source); SF "always fairly obvious what you need to do"
  https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/split-fiction-review/ (prior file).
- **Hidden inputs still slip through:** an ITT train-station section needed the partner to mash an action button with no
  prompt on screen, and players got stuck. https://forums.ea.com/discussions/ea-originals-discussion-en/it-takes-two---stuck-at-train-station/7630959
  (search summary) [verify]

## 3. Keeping both players busy and equal

- **Engagement imbalance is fixed by tuning, not by swapping:** in the Tree the match player was under-used, so enemies
  were made to attack that side more. https://80.lv/articles/hazelight-studios-working-culture-and-approach-to-creating-collaborative-gameplay
- **No role swap in a playthrough.** Each player keeps their lead for the whole game; swapping is what a second run is
  for. https://www.well-played.com.au/it-takes-two-review/ (prior file); https://hardcoregamer.com/review-split-fiction/
- **Balance as an arc, not a moment.** A 2026 thesis with 30 players across three SF segments found balance comes from
  alternating who "authors" conditions (opens routes, sets timing) and who "executes" inside them, across segments; at any
  one moment roles are unequal. Players remembered segments by who carried responsibility, not by the spatial trick.
  Breakdowns came from mistimed handovers and mismatched pace. https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2063623/FULLTEXT01.pdf
- **The authoring role feels heavier:** players in the tree-form segment said the route-shaping player felt "in charge"
  and responsible for the partner's safety; the executing player reported waiting and reacting. Same source.
- **Remaining gaps players noticed:**
  - SF: one writer often got the more fun power (flying acid dragon vs ground-bound dragon) "in multiple levels".
    https://hardcoregamer.com/review-split-fiction/
  - ITT: one player saw "a huge imbalance" favouring Lead 2's powers https://backloggd.com/u/Acetyou/reviews/ ; others
    found both equal in every world https://gamegrin.com/articles/wives-play-it-takes-two and "never left standing
    around" https://qa2.www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/it-takes-two-review (search summary).
  - Reddit thread (about 25 comments): one comment said some powers are less fun for one player. https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1swxvhl/snapshots/2026-04-27T15%3A47%3A26.535906Z
- **Idle-time tricks:**
  - Moving vehicles and chases leave no room to idle; the drive-and-shoot pairing keeps both hands busy (one steers, one
    shoots). https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1768861/FULLTEXT01.pdf
  - Downed player is not idle: they mash or hold to speed their own return. https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 ;
    https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/split-fiction-7-beginner-tips-tricks
  - Optional play objects and minigames sit along the route (ITT had 25 marked with floating purple tambourines; finding
    them unlocks them in the menu). https://www.powerpyx.com/it-takes-two-all-minigame-locations/
- **Playtest test for idleness:** staff watch pairs and flag any section cleared without communicating.
  https://80.lv/articles/hazelight-studios-working-culture-and-approach-to-creating-collaborative-gameplay

## 4. Staying together

- **Always split, both machines.** Online play still shows both halves. https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/03/24/it-takes-two-review/ ;
  Fares: they are "a couch co-op game that you can play online".
  https://www.theringer.com/2021/04/22/video-games/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming
- **Why:** Hazelight's communication design assumes you can see the partner's screen (Fares, via search summary
  https://www.inverse.com/gaming/it-takes-two-split-fiction-josef-fares-hazelight [verify exact wording]); a player defending
  it on Steam cited easier teamwork without talking and not missing the partner's scenes.
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/2001120/discussions/0/601898176917554670/
- **But players do not look.** The SF finale designer called "screen peeking" the hardest problem: players stayed locked
  on their own character. Fixes were blunt: passive traversal (a long bridge, a carrying flower), locked cameras that
  frame both worlds identically, and dialogue pointing at the other screen. https://www.invisiblefriends.net/gdc-2026-a-personal-account/
  Game Developer's write-up frames it as encouraging screen peeking. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/split-fiction-s-final-level-concept-was-originally-meant-for-the-whole-game
- **Split shape:** ITT keeps a steadier split than A Way Out's dynamic camera, by Fares' choice, with movement "in
  places". https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=375927 . Splits sometimes go 25:75 and some sections are one shared screen.
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/1426210/discussions/0/3077621289880725367 ; one player's view can fill the screen to
  stress their action https://mobygames.com/game/161320/it-takes-two/reviews/ (search summary). SF merges to one screen in
  some chases and 2D sections https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 ; the split line moves and changes angle in chases.
  https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/03/05/split-fiction-josef-fares-interview/ (search summary)
- **FOV / sickness:** narrow-view and motion-sickness complaints with no option to turn split off online.
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/1426210/discussions/0/3077621289882944908 . SF added a per-player persistent centre dot
  and camera-shake toggle, and no motion blur or chromatic aberration in play. https://www.ea.com/able/resources/split-fiction/split-fiction
- **Partner indicators:** neither game is reported to use partner markers or a compass; the split screen is the partner
  indicator [Inference; no source found for a marker]. A downed partner's half turns grey and their bar jumps to the
  centre of their half, which also tells the other player. https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1768861/FULLTEXT01.pdf
- **Stopping drift:** puzzles that need both and no backtracking https://80.lv/articles/hazelight-studios-working-culture-and-approach-to-creating-collaborative-gameplay ;
  chases pull both through checkpoints together https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 ; a hard tether in one section (too
  far apart = both die) https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/split-fiction-system-fail-safe-mode-walkthrough ; "double
  interact" blockers (disguised as pressure plates or stumps) and double doors that hide camera alignment.
  https://www.invisiblefriends.net/gdc-2026-a-personal-account/
- **Never trigger on one player's position.** Fares: you do not know where the partner is; cutscene timing depends on
  both. https://www.theringer.com/2021/04/22/video-games/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming
- **Spatial awareness becomes social:** players watch each other's position and hesitation and delay actions until both
  are ready. https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2063623/FULLTEXT01.pdf

## 5. Failure

- **Respawn rules (ITT):** fall or die outside combat = gone in a puff and back at a nearby checkpoint almost at once.
  In combat, a health bar appears; when you die your half greys and you press quickly to come back sooner; if the
  partner also dies first, both restart the encounter. No lives. https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1768861/FULLTEXT01.pdf ;
  https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/03/24/it-takes-two-review/
- **Exceptions that hurt:** vehicle sections used **shared health**; a plane section killed on contact and restarted from
  the start, which the thesis authors felt as "great disappointment". Same diva-portal source.
- **SF:** instant respawn at the nearest checkpoint outside fights; in bosses a meter fills by mashing (or holding the
  interact button, sources differ) while the partner keeps fighting; both down = last checkpoint; setbacks rarely more
  than about a minute. https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/split-fiction-7-beginner-tips-tricks ; https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 ;
  https://gamecritics.com/nyxatknight/split-fiction-review/
- **Design reason:** shared health was rejected because a weaker player drags the stronger one down; "It's fun to die...
  but it's not fun to redo it." (prior file, GMTK)
- **Death feel:** quick and a little comic, not punishing; "no real punishment for failure".
  https://wccftech.com/review/it-takes-two-love-multi-genre/ (search summary); one review: you can always come back while
  the partner lives and checkpoints are generous even in bosses. https://www.gamereactor.eu/it-takes-two-review/ (search summary)
- **Difficulty:** one setting in both games. https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/677272-it-takes-two-change-difficulty-easy-mode ;
  https://sportskeeda.com/esports/can-change-difficulty-split-fiction . SF is "a little bit more difficult" by design
  (Fares). https://videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/split-fiction-game-interview
- **Skill gap is the real difficulty problem:** Steam reviewers' top ITT difficulty complaint is "uneven,
  partner-dependent difficulty" https://vaporlens.app/app/1426210/it_takes_two.md ; for SF "difficulty causes
  frustration in co-op" is the second most frequent negative theme https://vaporlens.app/app/2001120/split_fiction.md .
  Fares warned a partner new to camera sticks would find it tough (search summary of a preview)
  https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/hands-on-it-takes-two-may-be-2021s-biggest-surprise/ [verify].
- **Assists (SF):** reduced enemy damage (stops some attacks killing you); skip to next checkpoint, with a confirmation
  and no achievement penalty; mash, spin and wiggle prompts can become holds or auto-succeed; per-player remapping,
  stick swap, camera assistance (auto rotation), aim dot; pause at any time.
  https://www.ea.com/able/resources/split-fiction/split-fiction ; https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173 ;
  https://gamingbolt.com/split-fictions-accessibility-options-include-skippable-checkpoints
- **Assists (ITT):** colour-blind modes and brightness/contrast are listed [verify; EA page looks generic]
  https://www.ea.com/able/resources/it-takes-two/pc/accessibility-settings ; no skip or damage assist found.
- **Accessibility gaps:** SF needs many separate buttons (interact, jump, dash, grapple, sprint, weapon, gravity shift,
  four driving directions); bosses demand many inputs and perspective changes; small subtitles with no size option.
  https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173

## 6. Prompts and UI

- **On-world prompts** above the character: button icon plus a short title; per-player colour; not colour-only.
  https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173
- **Two-person "ready" UI:** skipping a cutscene needs both players to hold a button; each fills half a circle and the
  skip fires when the halves meet. https://twinfinite.net/guides/it-takes-two-how-skip-cutscenes-dialogue/ (search summary)
- **Health only when it matters:** the health bar appears only when combat starts, which also warns the players.
  https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1768861/FULLTEXT01.pdf
- **Goal markers in open hubs only:** light poles and floating UI over each bell tower; voice prompts only when you first
  get near, which a thesis found too sparse. Same source.
- **Subtitles on both halves** when anyone speaks outside cutscenes. https://caniplaythat.com/?p=15173
- **Not on screen** [Inference from the sources above and no contrary source]: no minimap, no compass, no persistent
  objective list, no partner marker, no score during play.
- **Weak spot:** SF Steam reviews bundle "unclear objectives" with skill-gap frustration (0.63 weight).

## Lessons for Night Shift

Context: one stealth ruleset all campaign, online first with local split-screen, CT-style co-op, 20-30 minute missions.

| # | Tag | Lesson | How it works in Night Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adopt | Only movement and the core ruleset carry over; the *situation* changes | Keep the stealth kit fixed; each mission adds one situation rule (a tether, a blackout, a two-world view) for one stretch, not a new control |
| 2 | Adopt | Fairness is an arc: alternate who "authors" and who "executes" | Tag every beat with author/executor per player and check the mission alternates; the light-holder vs mover swap already fits |
| 3 | Adapt | Fix a dull role by tuning pressure, not by adding buttons | If the overwatch player is bored, give guards and cameras reasons to threaten that side too |
| 4 | Adopt | Teach in four steps: solo safe use, one handover, a hold, then both under time pressure | Marlow Yard order per team move; each first use has no guard able to see it |
| 5 | Adopt | Lock halves to the right player when a beat needs two | Use reach, position, carried item or a per-player target colour plus icon, never a shared prompt both can press |
| 6 | Adopt | Lone death is cheap and quick; only joint failure costs progress; never shared health | Downed = partner revive; both down = checkpoint; no shared bar; vehicle-style shared health avoided |
| 7 | Adapt | Keep the downed player busy | Let the downed player spectate the partner, ping and do a hold to slow bleed-out, so they are not idle |
| 8 | Adopt | Lost progress under about a minute where possible | Add soft checkpoints at each 2-4 minute beat, not only per area; stealth replays cost more than platforming replays |
| 9 | Adopt | One difficulty plus assists: reduced damage, skip-to-checkpoint with confirmation, mash-to-hold, per-player remap, aim dot, camera shake off | Skip-to-checkpoint should cap the rating, not block achievements |
| 10 | Adopt | Two-person "ready" UI: each player fills half a ring | Use for cutscene skip, mission start, two-key actions and extraction |
| 11 | Adopt | Prompt above the character: icon + short title + player colour, never colour alone | Matches our "prompt names the move and who starts it" |
| 12 | Adopt | Never trigger on one player's position; gate on both or on world state | Already in plan; also apply to radio lines and music changes |
| 13 | Adopt | Split information, split execution, finish with timing | Formula for each mission's big set piece (one sees the guard pattern, the other has the access, both act on a count) |
| 14 | Adapt | Experiments live in short optional side content | Optional 5-15 minute side ops can try one-off ideas without breaking the main ruleset; this gives variety without a carousel |
| 15 | Adopt | Save the most expensive idea for one late level | One late mission with a unique twist, seeded early |
| 16 | Avoid | Bosses that are "do X three times" | Climaxes are joint stealth set pieces with a twist |
| 17 | Avoid | Hidden two-player inputs with no prompt | Every partner action has a visible prompt and a partner-ready sign |
| 18 | Avoid | Tutorial-heavy, cutscene-heavy openings | Cap the prologue teaching; let play start inside a minute |
| 19 | Adapt | Optional competitive minigames are valued (players missed them in SF) | Raise our in-fiction side bits from "Could" to a cheap "Should" |
| 20 | Adopt | Budget rendering for two views and skip costly features | Local split doubles cost; profile Babylon with two cameras from the first greybox |
| 21 | Adopt | Fast iteration loop is the studio's real engine | Keep hot reload in the dev build; prototype a team move in days, polish later |
| 22 | Adapt | Pair-test for silence and for skill gap | Pair a strong and a weak player in every playtest; log silent stretches and who waited |

### What challenges our current plan
1. **Full screen each online vs Hazelight's always-split.** Hazelight insists on it and players who defend it cite
   coordination. Against: requests to turn it off recur (small, about 0.03 of ITT reviews); Hazelight's own GDC talk says
   players mostly do not look at the other half; and in stealth, seeing the partner's view would leak the information
   asymmetry we want (one sees the guard, must call it out). Full screen also halves our online render cost.
   **Recommendation:** keep full screen each online, but add a hold-to-glance partner view or a small partner
   picture-in-picture during holds and overwatch, and make pings carry what split screen would show. Note Fares told
   GamesRadar a co-op Splinter Cell should be "split-screen only" https://www.gamesradar.com/games/co-op/split-fiction-director-josef-fares-says-ubisoft-is-struggling-and-should-make-a-co-op-splinter-cell-game-dont-chicken-out-to-do-single-player-just-say-this-is-split-screen-only/ ;
   that is about local play and shipping co-op only, which we already do. Decision for Michael.
2. **Revive limits.** Hazelight has none; a lone death is nearly free, and the cost is time, not a counter. Evidence
   favours **no revive limit**, with the cost carried by stealth consequences (noise, alert level, rating) and the
   partner's walk to you. Decide before Marlow Yard.
3. **Gear choice.** Hazelight's dependency comes from *assigned* asymmetric tools; they never let players pick.
   Free loadouts risk both picking the same gadgets, which removes the "only you can do this half" lock. Options:
   two kit slots that must differ, or a mission-suggested pair with free swaps. Also check that our symmetric team
   moves still give each player a different job each beat.
4. **Compass and objectives.** Hazelight shows no compass, minimap or partner marker and relies on linear levels and
   split screen; yet "unclear objectives" is part of SF's biggest complaint. With full-screen online and 2-3 routes per
   room, our compass and partner mark are justified; keep the level doing the guiding as planned.
5. **Checkpoint spacing.** Our "start of each area and before each set piece" can cost several minutes in a 20-30 minute
   mission. Hazelight rarely costs more than a minute. Consider soft checkpoints at every beat.
6. **No mechanic carousel.** Player data likes variety more than our rule assumes. Our answer must be visible variety
   in situations (lesson 1), optional side ops (14) and one late twist (15), or reviews will call missions samey.
7. **Role swaps.** Hazelight never swaps characters mid-run but alternates author/executor by design. Our "players swap
   roles" should mean the beat structure alternates, not a swap button. Confirm the wording with Michael.
8. **Length.** ITT was called too long at 11-12 h for co-op only; SF at 10-14 h had a slow start. Plan the campaign
   around about 6-8 h, which matches the A Way Out lesson already taken.
9. **Pause online.** SF lets you pause any time. Our plan does not say; a stealth game online must decide whether pause
   freezes both (Hazelight) or not.
