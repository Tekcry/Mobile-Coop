## 1. A Way Out, chapter by chapter

Structure: 5 acts, 37 chapters; chapters run from a few minutes to about 30 minutes; 6-8 hours, about 10 to see both
routes. https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/378215-a-way-out-how-many-chapters-how-long
The prison act alone is about 2 hours. https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
Acts 2-5 lengths below are [Inference] from chapter counts and walkthroughs.

Both leads have identical functions; personality comes through animation and dialogue only.
https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/13/a-fascinating-co-op-narrative

## 2. Split-screen storytelling

What it does
- Split screen only, local and online, "from day one"; not a port of a single-player design. https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
- The split moves between vertical, horizontal and sweeping layouts; one chase passes control between players in one
  continuous shot. https://thesixthaxis.com/2018/03/22/a-way-out-review
- The more active player gets more screen (the driver gets most, the passenger less); the halves merge when both are in
  one place. https://sirusgaming.com/what-makes-couch-coop-work-for-a-way-out/
- One player can be in a cutscene while the other plays; Fares accepted that some story would be missed.
  https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
- Fares: the hard part is knowing "where both players are"; cutscenes must make both focus.
  https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2018/03/13/a-way-out-director-josef-fares-on-the-difference-between-video-games-and-movies.aspx
- Events cannot be fired at a point the way a single-player game does, and cutting to an animation exposes "popping" on
  the other screen. https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming
- Online split screen meant you always see your friend's view. Same source.

What worked
- The hospital chapter, one continuous shot following both leads, was the best co-op execution. Watching both halves is
  not distracting. https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/22/game-informer-a-way-out-review
- Split view pays off most in puzzles, where seeing both perspectives matters. https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/a-way-out-review
- Couch players comment on each other's half in real time. https://sirusgaming.com/what-makes-couch-coop-work-for-a-way-out/

What did not
- "A gimmick, instead of a real storytelling device"; dual views underused in story scenes.
  https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/a-way-out-review
- Players miss details on their own side. https://thesixthaxis.com/2018/03/22/a-way-out-review
- Two simultaneous conversations ducked under each other; sometimes the less important one wins.
  https://thesixthaxis.com/2018/03/22/a-way-out-review ; https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/22/game-informer-a-way-out-review
- Fares later: players talk through cutscenes and miss content; co-op story is the hardest part.
  https://www.inverse.com/gaming/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview
- Steam: long cutscenes interrupt play (14 mentions). https://vaporlens.app/app/1222700

## 3. Two temperaments

How they are written
- Overt lead: rash, violent, "charming simplicity". Strategic lead: cautious, avoids conflict, fights when needed.
  https://thesixthaxis.com/2018/03/22/a-way-out-review
- Characters will not act against their personality, which limits player freedom on purpose.
  https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2017/06/13/the-uncompromising-vision-of-co-op-title-a-way-out
- NPCs react differently depending on which lead you play. https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
- Hazelight's pairs are always contrasts: one guarded and pessimistic, one open and optimistic.
  https://www.inverse.com/gaming/josef-fares-interview-split-fiction-it-takes-two-hazelight-coop
- Fares: "You need a conflict for it to be interesting." https://gamesbeat.com/how-josef-fares-stayed-focused-on-the-co-op-action-adventure-with-split-fiction-interview/

How they become play
- About 8 joint choices (section 1). Each is labelled with a lead's name; both must pick the same one; "there is no
  middle way". https://epiloguegaming.com/collaboration-cooperation-and-contention-in-a-way-out/
- Each choice changes the gameplay scenario (drive vs sneak, scare vs subdue, bluff vs threaten), not the story.
  https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
- Loud and quiet routes can differ in consequence inside the scene (the loud gas-station route risks a 911 call and a
  police timer). https://shacknews.com/article/103874/a-way-out---chapter-3-preparation

Player reaction
- Liked: the negotiation itself, and an "I-told-you-so" rivalry between partners.
  https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/13/a-fascinating-co-op-narrative
- Disliked: "you end up at the same places no matter what"; choices do not touch the relationship.
  https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/22/game-informer-a-way-out-review ;
  "no effect on the outcome" https://shacknews.com/article/103892/a-way-out-review-bring-a-friend ; Steam: choices have
  little impact (7 mentions, minor). https://vaporlens.app/app/1222700
- Choices mostly appeal as "how does the other way play", not as consequence.
  https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/2018/03/14/way-trophies-reveal-story-details-platinum/ [verify]
- The pair lacks banter-built bond; the relationship feels one of convenience and play does not strengthen it.
  https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/22/game-informer-a-way-out-review

## 4. The endings

### A Way Out
- Setup: hours of agreeing on the same paths build trust that mirrors the leads' friendship.
  https://www.theringer.com/2018/04/16/video-games/a-way-out-ending-merits-of-interactive-storytelling
- Mechanics: hostage scene, helicopter vs boat, warehouse gunfight with health bars, fist fight, crawl to the gun. The
  player who took less damage reaches it first: "the game rewards competency, as opposed to collaboration".
  https://epiloguegaming.com/collaboration-cooperation-and-contention-in-a-way-out/
- The winner is then made to shoot the unarmed partner; they hold hands; the survivor later visits the dead man's family.
  https://www.theringer.com/2018/04/16/video-games/a-way-out-ending-merits-of-interactive-storytelling ; two endings,
  one per lead, no hidden third. https://primagames.com/tips/a-way-out-all-endings-guide (search snippet) [verify]
- Fares knew the beginning and the end before development started, and enjoys that people call it both best and worst
  ending. https://embed.gamereactor.eu/josef-fares-i-love-to-fck-with-peoples-minds/
- Why it landed: the cooperation makes the betrayal personal; players feel the turn through their hands.
  https://epiloguegaming.com/collaboration-cooperation-and-contention-in-a-way-out/ ; streams show gasps, silence and
  tears; the peak is the final moments, not the reveal. https://kotaku.com/watching-people-react-to-a-way-outs-ending-is-too-good-1824119464
- Steam: "emotional and impactful" 53 mentions vs "unsatisfying and illogical" 36. https://vaporlens.app/app/1222700
- Criticisms:
  - Thin leads mean "it's hard to actually care who wins". https://www.avclub.com/a-way-out-saved-its-best-idea-for-the-last-20-minutes-1826028147
  - The twist is revealed too late to build tension; the versus section is squeezed into the last stretch.
    https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/a-way-outs-ending-sucked
  - The forced execution removes agency: "There wasn't a way out after all." The secret-agent player could have been
    told earlier to make him complicit. https://www.theringer.com/2018/04/16/video-games/a-way-out-ending-merits-of-interactive-storytelling
  - The secret agent killing many police clashes with his earlier calm choices. Same source.
  - Knowledge asymmetry can wreck it: one player who knew the twist and hid it angered a partner for days.
    https://kotaku.com/a-way-out-co-op-almost-ended-my-relationship-1851244415
  - A friend guessed it before the prison break. https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/a-way-outs-ending-sucked
  - Gives the story to the more skilful player. https://epiloguegaming.com/collaboration-cooperation-and-contention-in-a-way-out/

### Brothers
- Setup: the opening shows the mother drowning; the younger brother cannot swim, so the older carries him across water
  all game. https://avclub.com/brothers-embodies-the-best-of-siblinghood-when-it-break-1798281600
- Turn: the older brother is mortally wounded and dies; the player carries his body into the grave (interactive burial).
  https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons
- Payoff: the younger brother returns alone; at the water, holding the older brother's trigger (left side) lets him swim,
  pull levers and reach a ladder. https://kotaku.com/brothers-tale-of-two-sons-remake-ending-older-younger-1851302128
- Fares: left hand is the older brother so losing one feels like losing a hand; players were confused at first, so sound
  and vibration were added; he calls it the core of the game and an optimistic ending.
  https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons
- Reaction: "cut off your left hand" minutes before the end after three hours learning the scheme.
  https://www.snackbar-games.com/features/new-game-brothers-and-the-sibling-connection/ ; the game never tells you how to
  feel; your hands carry the grief. https://avclub.com/brothers-embodies-the-best-of-siblinghood-when-it-break-1798281600
- Why it lands [Inference]: the payoff is a control the player has used hundreds of times; nothing new to learn; the
  meaning is in the absence.

## 5. Brothers (2013): two characters, one pad

- Left stick + left trigger = older brother; right stick + right trigger = younger brother; trigger is the only action.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons
- Abilities differ: older is strong (levers, boosts), younger is small (through bars). Same source.
- Personality in shared interactions: the older sweeps with a broom, the younger balances it to make someone laugh;
  the older talks to a gardener, the younger kicks him. https://www.engadget.com/2012-10-30-playing-with-age-in-starbreezes-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons.html
- Puzzles tuned for comfort on one pad (rope swings, carrying logs); like tying your own shoelaces.
  https://shacknews.com/article/81361/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-not-meant-for-co ;
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/a-film-director-gets-video-games-right-in-starbreeze-s-i-brothers-i-
- Distract-and-pass appears here first: one brother distracts a dog while the other moves.
  https://www.engadget.com/2012-10-30-playing-with-age-in-starbreezes-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons.html
- Wordless: an invented language, no subtitles, no text beyond a control hint; players read body language.
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/a-film-director-gets-video-games-right-in-starbreeze-s-i-brothers-i-
- Fares refused co-op: he would "rather cut my hands"; publishers and the platform holder pushed for it.
  https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/09/27/afterwords-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons
- When co-op was added (Switch 2019, then the 2024 remake): reviewers said it reduces play to "walking around and
  pressing the action button" https://cogconnected.com/review/brothers-switch-review/ ; others liked the talk it
  creates with a child https://gamecritics.com/brian-theisen/brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-switch-review/ ; the remake
  "warns you away from" it. https://kotaku.com/brothers-tale-of-two-sons-remake-ending-older-younger-1851302128
- Criticism: zigzagging brothers, clumsy controls make emotional scenes feel "stupid", teamwork stressed heavy-handedly.
  https://haywiremag.com/columns/off-the-grid-brothers/
- Short (3-4 h) on purpose; "everything is unique". https://www.engadget.com/2012-10-30-playing-with-age-in-starbreezes-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons.html
- BAFTA Innovation 2014; 800k by Jan 2015; Metacritic PC 90. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers:_A_Tale_of_Two_Sons

Lessons for a two-player game [Inference]
- Give each character one verb-set that the player owns physically; the bond lives in the input, not the cutscene.
- When two humans split one design meant for one pair of hands, the puzzles get trivially easy unless redesigned for
  communication. Our game is two humans, so the puzzles must need talk, not dexterity.

## 6. Character and writing craft across Hazelight games

Interleaving story and play
- "Whatever happens in the story will be reflected in the gameplay." https://www.theringer.com/2021/4/22/22396763/josef-fares-it-takes-two-interview-co-op-gaming
- Weapons only when "the story allows you to"; about half a dozen kills each in A Way Out.
  https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2018/03/13/a-way-out-director-josef-fares-on-the-difference-between-video-games-and-movies.aspx ;
  https://gamingbolt.com/an-interview-with-josef-fares-the-creator-of-a-way-out-i-always-knew-we-had-something-special
- A mechanic appears only when it fits the scene; one side-scrolling fight appears once.
  https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2017/06/13/the-uncompromising-vision-of-co-op-title-a-way-out
- Problem set-up then solving phase, no backtracking (carried from A Way Out to It Takes Two).
  https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2021/02/12/splitting-up-together
- Fares tries to keep control during cutscenes. https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2018/03/13/a-way-out-director-josef-fares-on-the-difference-between-video-games-and-movies.aspx
- Mechanics as metaphor (broken magnet = lost attraction; time and clone = no time together).
  https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/03/02/interview-josef-fares-on-it-takes-two-relationship-advice-co-operation-favourite-swear-words/

What reviewers praised
- Lovable leads, friendship visibly growing. https://shacknews.com/article/103892/a-way-out-review-bring-a-friend
- Endearing, well-acted overt lead. https://gameinformer.com/games/a_way_out/b/playstation4/archive/2018/03/22/game-informer-a-way-out-review
- Steam: story engaging and well written (53 mentions). https://vaporlens.app/app/1222700
- Split Fiction side stories "true charm"; lighter tone than the main plot. https://gamingbolt.com/split-fiction-review-split-quality

What reviewers criticised
- Thin archetypes, hammy cliched dialogue, emotional seeds that never take root (A Way Out).
  https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/a-way-out-review ; predictable story https://shacknews.com/article/103892/a-way-out-review-bring-a-friend ;
  Eurogamer: fresh presentation "can't save" it from mediocrity https://opencritic.com/game/5413/view
- Humour in set pieces undercuts later twists (tonal whiplash). https://thesixthaxis.com/2018/03/22/a-way-out-review
- Family diversions do not explain why the revenge plot is worth the risk. https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/a-way-out-review
- It Takes Two: one scene where the parents destroy their daughter's toy was called tonally jarring; Fares defended it as
  showing how self-centred they had become. https://opencritic.com/news/13540/that-stuffed-elephant-you-murdered-in-it-takes-two-makes-an-appearance-in-split-fiction
- The comic helper character: Fares calls him deliberately cheesy, "a psychopath with a heart", whose lines "have a
  point". https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/03/02/interview-josef-fares-on-it-takes-two-relationship-advice-co-operation-favourite-swear-words/
  Reviewers found him irritating (research file A).
- Split Fiction: "a constant stream of chatter", "tired cliches", one-dimensional villain, melodrama.
  https://gamingbolt.com/split-fiction-review-split-quality ; banter built on Marvel-style quips (user and blog reviews)
  https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1swxvhl/snapshots/2026-04-27T15%3A47%3A26.535906Z [verify]

Dialogue density [Inference from the above]
- A Way Out: talk mostly in scenes and optional NPC chats; criticised for too little banter.

## Lessons for Night Shift

Context: two operators, Wren and Moth; nine nights; dry banter; text radio only for now; one joint approach choice per
mission at most; 20-30 minute missions; forced co-op stealth.

| # | Lesson | Tag | How it works in Night Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Know the ending before building (Brothers and A Way Out fixed start and end on day one) | Adopt | Write Night 9's final scene and its 3-4 seeds in Phase 1, before Mission 1 is built |
| 2 | The ending lives in the controls the players have used all game (Brothers) | Adopt | Night 9's climax uses the handshake team move under the hardest conditions: e.g. one operator pinned and unable to move, the other must complete both halves of a move alone using a callback cue the pair learned on Night 1; or a final two-key action across distance where only the radio connects them |
| 3 | Do not turn the players on each other at the end (A Way Out's PvP split players 53 vs 36 on Steam; skill decides the story) | Avoid | The finale stays forced co-op; the emotional turn is a shared loss or sacrifice, never a versus fight |
| 4 | No secret story knowledge held by one player | Avoid | Both get the same story text; information asymmetry is for gameplay (who sees the guard), never for twists |
| 5 | Same abilities, different temperaments (A Way Out leads are mechanically identical) | Adopt | Wren and Moth share one kit; temperament shows in banter, idle animations and the labelled joint choice |
| 6 | Joint approach choice labelled by character, both must agree, no middle way | Adopt | "Wren's way" (overt, faster, louder risk) vs "Moth's way" (patient, longer, safer); choice shown as both players' picks; locks only when both match |
| 7 | Choices that change nothing get called out | Adapt | Keep "changes route, not story", but add one cheap echo: a banter callback next night, and a change in the next mission's starting alert or a guard who remembers |
| 8 | Cutscene for one, play for the other, but players miss story | Adapt | Story beats on text radio go to both screens; a story beat for one operator gives the other a short overwatch task with no new dialogue at the same moment |
| 9 | Two conversations at once were unreadable | Adopt | One radio line at a time across both players; important lines queue, chatter drops; every line tagged with an owner (Hazelight's "which player is this sound for") |
| 10 | Banter density: too little (A Way Out) and too much (Split Fiction) both criticised | Adopt | Banter reacts to what players just did; silent on alert; one exchange per calm stretch; nothing that solves a puzzle |
| 11 | Quiet beats are the most loved (fishing, waterfall bench, duet) | Adapt | One 1-2 minute optional quiet beat per night, in fiction (safehouse, rooftop wait, shared smoke at the exit) with a both-press interaction and a banter reward |
| 12 | Story beat reflected in play ("whatever happens in the story... in the gameplay") | Adopt | Each night's story turn changes the mission rule or space (e.g. Moth is wounded: Wren carries the loud jobs) |
| 13 | The one-take hospital escape: split up, same camera, both visible | Adapt | A split-and-sync step where each player's compass shows the partner's live progress and a short partner-cam inset appears |
| 14 | Online split screen means you always see your partner | Adapt | Optional small partner view (picture-in-picture) at hold-and-pass and split moments only; costs a second render so test it in the Babylon spike |
| 15 | A big scene used once | Adapt | One set piece per night may be unique, built from the existing stealth verbs; no new controls (keeps our anti-carousel rule) |
| 16 | Late forced shooting galleries were the weakest part | Avoid | No mandatory firefight; the climax is still stealth |
| 17 | Characters need a clear arc; contrasting pairs | Adopt | Wren: certain, impatient, learns to wait; Moth: careful, guarded, learns to trust. One line each in the story bible |
| 18 | Comic helper who spells things out; tonal whiplash | Avoid | No third funny character; HQ voice is dry and brief; no slapstick in the techno-thriller tone |
| 19 | Pair-test: friends play a section, staff watch for silent sections and "did they know they cooperated" | Adopt | Michael and partner test each milestone; plus one fresh pair per mission; log talk/ping count per step |
| 20 | Story beats first, but story bends to play | Adapt | Mission beat sheet first (why, stakes, what changes for Wren and Moth); if a step is not fun, rewrite the story beat, not the stealth rules |
| 21 | Prototype fast; judge early whether it can reach polish; cut 20-30% | Adopt | Already in plan; add a "polish-able?" yes/no to each prototype review |
| 22 | Owner hosts; one copy for two; the free copy was a word-of-mouth driver | Adopt | Already in plan; the guest's progress should carry over if they buy (Split Fiction) |
| 23 | Session ends when a player drops (A Way Out) | Avoid | Reconnect to the last checkpoint with the same partner; no solo fallback, but no lost progress |
| 24 | Short tutorials, momentum for both | Adopt | Marlow Yard steps each under 2 minutes; teach by doing |
| 25 | Shared music track for both players | Adopt | One adaptive score state for the pair, per research file E; stingers owned by the player who caused them |

### Things that challenge our current plan
1. **Campaign length.** Nine nights at 20-30 minutes is about 3-4.5 hours, half of A Way Out (6-8 h) at a similar price
   point, and Chaos Theory's biggest complaint was a short campaign. Decide the price and length together, or add
   nights or longer final missions.
2. **"An ending that uses the bond"** was taken from A Way Out, but A Way Out did it by turning players against each
   other, which breaks our one rule (forced co-op). The plan should say the ending stays co-operative and point to
   Brothers' model (meaning through a familiar control) instead.
3. **"One joint approach choice per mission, changes route not story"** copies the exact feature critics called
   consequence-free. Keep it cheap but add a visible echo (next-night banter, starting alert, a remembered guard).
4. **Text radio only** makes the cutscene-for-one pattern harder: text on one screen is easy to miss and two text
   streams at once repeat A Way Out's overlapping-audio problem. Needs a rule: one line at a time, shared to both
   screens, queue by priority.
5. **No partner view online.** Hazelight always shows the partner's screen, even online, and leans on it for drama
   and awareness. Our plan gives each player a full screen. Consider a small partner view for key moments (render cost
   to test).
6. **No between-mission quiet space.** A Way Out's best-loved beats are quiet interludes. The plan has no hub or
   safehouse; a short optional interlude per night is cheap and builds the bond the ending needs.
7. **Story-first vs Hazelight's practice.** Our plan says story first; Hazelight's mature process is beats first, then
   play, with story yielding. Not a conflict, but the plan should say who wins a tie (play, per pillar ranking).
8. **Identical kits.** Hazelight's first co-op game used identical kits and still read as two people. If the plan
   later drifts to giving Wren and Moth different gadgets, check it against "nobody waits, roles swap".
