# The 12 proposed level-rule changes (G157), plain English - Campaign Build, 2026-10-25 ~18:05

Source: docs/ct-research/design-kit-digest.md appendix (written 2026-10-18), checked against today's rules.

1. Built for two: every area is designed for the pair, it cannot be done by one player, every two-person spot has a real-world reason, and at least one climb per mission needs teamwork. Why: the campaign is two players only. In play: no area can be done alone. Status: ALREADY A RULE (G2, G253, G292; Q23 throw gaps force co-op).
2. Team moves anywhere: boosts and other team moves work wherever there is room, not only at marked spots. Why: the old rules disagreed. In play: players can boost on any suitable wall. Status: ALREADY A RULE (G151).
3. Revive window: a downed player can be revived within 30 s, twice per mission; both down = checkpoint restart. Why: replaces the old "no bleed-out" line. In play: the partner must get there fast. Status: ALREADY A RULE (G126).
4. Teamwork minimums: each mission has a minimum of each kind of teamwork (doors held, boosts, covering a partner, a split that ends in a joint action); nobody waits more than 60 s; players swap roles; joint actions are forgiving, work with pings alone and raise the alarm on failure instead of killing. Why: constant, varied teamwork. In play: always something to do together. Status: PARTLY (splits meet up within 90 s, G284; a forced team move every section, G292).
5. Pacing in minutes: a small teamwork moment every 2-4 min, a big one every 6-10 min, 30-90 s of calm after each big one, 1-2 signature moments never repeated, plus one objective twist 40-60% of the way through. Why: today's pacing rule has no clock. In play: a steady rhythm and a mid-mission surprise. Status: PARTLY (tension rises and falls by area; minutes and twist are new).
6. Lookouts for two: the safe lookout at each area's entrance fits both players, and the final area can be seen from a distance before reaching it. Why: both plan together; seeing the finale early builds anticipation. In play: both crouch at one window. Status: PARTLY (entry lookout and far landmarks exist; room for two and seeing the finale early are new).
7. Readable world: machines show on or off by look and sound with a visible link to their switch; a lit window exposes whoever is in it from both sides; weather affects light and sound; harmless noises are clearly harmless. Why: fair stealth. In play: players can tell what a switch does and what is a real threat. Status: PARTLY (lit windows decided, G144, not yet in the level rules; the rest is new).
8. Two-minute test: at every blocked route, players can see or work out a way past from where they stand within about 2 minutes. Why: with no maps or markers, the level itself must show the answer. In play: less wandering lost. Status: NEW (supports G336 / G339).
9. Designed exit: the run to the exit takes under 2 min, or uses a different route for at least half the way back; both players must reach it. Why: retracing steps is dull. In play: a quick, fresh getaway. Status: PARTLY (both reach the exit, G138; the short or different route is new).
10. Real building safety: two separate staircases in every multi-storey building; exits open from inside; fire alarms unlock electric doors; realistic signs; cameras, sensors and beams placed like real ones; every security weak spot has a story reason (for example renovation work). Why: believable buildings. In play: weak spots make sense. Status: PARTLY (every device needs a reason, G295; building elements need a real reason).
11. Extra planning pages: each map adds a site security summary, an objective chain showing which steps need both players, three wow moments checked against five tests, and a plan of pings and warnings per team move. Why: catches gaps before building. In play: nothing directly; better-planned missions. Status: PARTLY (device list G295, objective and guidance plan G336; the rest is new).
12. Final map tests: two players can finish unseen; each player's goggle-light colour can be told apart at about 20 m in the darkest zone; a downed player can be revived within 30 s on every team-move surface. Why: proves the map works for two. In play: nothing directly. Status: PARTLY (unseen run and revive tests exist; G135, G126). "Darkest zone" clashes with Q2 "Never" dark spots: reword to "darkest real spot".

Recommended: 1, 2, 3 - already rules, just confirm. 4 - adopt reworded (keep the stricter 90 s split limit; size minimums to fit Tallow). 5 - adopt reworded (minutes are playtest targets, not hard rules). 6 - adopt. 7 - adopt reworded (weather only on maps that have weather). 8 - adopt. 9 - adopt. 10 - adopt reworded (fire alarms unlocking doors as a player tool gets its own decision). 11 - adopt reworded (add to existing plans, not new documents). 12 - adopt reworded ("darkest real spot").
